# The Balic Saddle

The broad low notch in the southern rim where the highland finally comes down — and where the silt, finding a way downhill, spills through in a wide gray ramp that stops dead against the rock that [Balic](Balic.md) is built into.

## At a glance

- **Height** A few hundred feet where the rest of the wall stands thousands. This is the gap.
- **What it means** It is the reason Balic exists, the reason Balic is rich, and the reason everything the south trades goes through one city's tolls.
- **The ramp** Gray silt descends the saddle in a broad chute between cliff shoulders and comes to rest above the city — held there by the rock, with the silt quays and moored skimmers at the top of it.
- **The crossing** Cargo goes up and over on human backs: the terraced crane galleries of the **Stair of Sails** on the outside, and the **Cochlea**, the great spiral stair bored down through the rock, on the inside.

## Traveler's notes

- Standing in Balic's ocean harbor you can look up and see the masts of silt schooners along the skyline. It is the strangest sight on the continent and the locals have stopped noticing it.
- Silt giants occasionally come *down* the ramp to raid the fields. The legions handle it; the fields get replanted; nobody moves away.
- The saddle is why the southern road exists at all. Cut it, and the entire south would have to go the long way round the ring.
