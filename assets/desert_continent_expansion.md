# The Desert Continent Expansion
## Dark Sun & Dune Lore Integration for the Barrier Peaks Campaign

> **World:** Vermoon. The Barrier Peaks campaign takes place in the **Scarlands** (the cold southern landmass where Greenfjord and Frostwatch Hold are located). The desert continent is **Aerun** — the equatorial landmass at the center of the known world, orange-sanded and sun-blasted, the Dune/Dark Sun analogue. Kaelen al-Hajra is from Aerun.

---

## Overview

The party has just defeated two mind flayers and four intellect devourers in the Level 2 theater. They stand on the threshold of the garden level, carrying red key cards, laser pistols, and the weight of everything they have seen. The threat inside the ship is no longer a local problem. It is a world-ending one — and someone from the other side of the world already knows it.

This document introduces **Kaelen al-Hajra**, a new companion NPC from the Aerun, and establishes two major plot threads that will carry the campaign forward: the **ecological memory of a dying world**, and the **political fracture forming against the Administrator's rule**. Both threads draw directly from the *Dark Sun* and *Dune* source materials in the project library, and both connect to existing campaign lore — Jak, the Spásistren, Greenfjord, and the void that Jak cannot see.

## New NPC: Kaelen al-Hajra

*"The desert does not lie. It shows you exactly what the world becomes when power is taken without giving anything back."*

### Concept

Kaelen is a **Dune Strider** — a wasteland nomad and primal shaman from **Aerun**, the desert continent of Vermoon. She is the product of a civilization that has already survived its apocalypse. Where the party is still learning that the world can end, Kaelen knows it intimately. She has walked across the ruins of the Green Age. She has read the story of a world's death in the sand.

She is not here by accident. She was sent.

### Background

The world of Vermoon is not entirely Greenfjord and the cold Scarlands where the Barrier Peaks reside. Across the sea to the north lies **Aerun** — a vast, sun-scorched desert continent, harsh and Dune-like, the remnant of what was once a lush and verdant land. The people who survived the desertification did not do so by building cities. They did so by learning to move, to read the land, and to listen to the primal spirits of the world.

Kaelen belongs to one of these nomadic tribes. Her people are called the **Sandwalkers** — a loose confederation of desert survivors who preserve oral histories stretching back to the Green Age. They are not scholars. They are witnesses. Their knowledge is carried in song, in scar, and in the careful reading of ruins.

Three months ago, the primal spirits of the deep desert began to scream.

The Sandwalkers have a name for what they felt: **the Defiling Silence**. It is the absence of sound that precedes the death of a place — the moment when the living energy of the land is being consumed faster than it can regenerate. They have heard this silence before, in the ruins of the old cities. They know what comes after it.

Kaelen was chosen to travel north, cross the sea, and find the source of the Defiling Silence. She arrived in the Barrier Peaks region weeks before the party. She has been watching the infection spread. She has been watching the mountains die.

She knows what the ship is. Not its technology — she has no framework for that. But she knows what it is *doing*. And she knows the party is the only force that has gone inside it and come back out.

### Connection to Existing Characters

Kaelen has no prior connection to the party, to Jak, or to George Decay. Her arrival is independent and, from Jak's perspective, invisible — the Sandwalkers have never accepted Aura implants, and Kaelen carries no technology that the network can track. She is, in Jak's terms, a ghost.

She has heard of George Decay, however. The Sandwalkers trade information across vast distances, and word of a scientist dying inside the alien vessel reached the desert long before the party arrived. She does not know him personally, but she respects what she has heard: a man who chose to keep thinking rather than stop feeling.

### Personality

Kaelen is **economical with words and generous with action**. She does not explain herself unless asked, and she does not ask for permission before doing what needs to be done. She has the quiet authority of someone who has survived things that would break most people — not because she is hard, but because she has made peace with the cost of survival.

She is deeply suspicious of the Aura network and the Spásistren. She has seen what happens when a single power controls the flow of information across a continent. She watched it happen to her own people's ancestors. She will not say this directly to the party, but she will say it about Jak if pressed, with the flat certainty of someone stating a geological fact.

She has a dry, desert-dark sense of humor that surfaces in unexpected moments. She is not cruel. She is honest.

**Signature phrases:**
- *"The sand remembers everything. The problem is that most people don't speak sand."*
- *"I've seen this before. Not this ship. This pattern."*
- *"Your friend George is dying. But he's dying with his eyes open. That matters."*
- *"The network sees what it's pointed at. The desert sees everything."*

**Roleplaying Kaelen**

Kaelen does not perform. She observes, then acts. When she speaks, it is worth listening to. She will not argue with the party, but she will state her position once, clearly, and then do what she believes is necessary regardless of consensus. She is not reckless — she is simply operating on a longer timeline than most people around her.

She is deeply moved by George Decay, though she will not show it openly. She recognizes in him the same quality she values in her own people: the refusal to stop being a person even when the body is failing. She will offer him a Sandwalker remedy — not a cure, but a stabilizer made from desert plants that can slow the infection's progression and reduce his pain. It will not save him, but it will give him more time and more clarity.

She is fascinated by the ship's technology in the way a geologist is fascinated by a fault line: not with wonder, but with the careful attention of someone who understands what it means when the ground moves.

## Plot Thread 1: The Green Age — A World That Died Before

### The Core Revelation

Aerun was not always a desert. Centuries ago — the exact timeline is disputed, and the Sandwalkers' oral histories are deliberately vague on dates — it was a living world. Rivers ran through valleys that are now dry canyons. Forests covered the hills that are now dune fields. Cities rose and fell, and their ruins are everywhere beneath the sand.

The desertification was not gradual. It happened within living memory of the oldest Sandwalker oral traditions — fast enough to be catastrophic, slow enough to be survived by those who adapted. The Sandwalkers' ancestors watched the world die around them and chose to move rather than fight what they could not stop.

The framework supports three possible explanations, all of which can coexist as competing theories:

| Theory | Source | Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Defiling Wars** | Ancient sorcerer-kings (Dark Sun parallel) used life-draining magic in a catastrophic war, consuming the land's vitality faster than it could regenerate | The Aerun's ruins contain dangerous residual energy; the ship's infection is a technological version of the same process |
| **The Fallen Star** | A vessel from the stars (a *second* crashed ship, or a precursor to the Barrier Peaks ship) impacted the Aerun long ago, leaking the same kind of energy drain | The Barrier Peaks ship is not unique; it is one of many; the Aerun is evidence of what happens when the infection wins |
| **The Titan's Fall** | The colossal entity known as the Titan (the "Work") was either the cause of the devastation or the last desperate attempt to stop it | The Titan still slumbers or influences the deep desert, and the ship's energy threatens to awaken or corrupt it |
| **The Sorcerer-King's Bargain** | A single powerful ruler made a pact with something from beyond the world, trading the land's vitality for personal immortality | The Administrator's role (and perhaps Jak's growing power) echoes this bargain; the Aura network may be draining something |

**DM Note:** The "Fallen Star" theory is the most directly useful for connecting the two plot threads, as it implies the Barrier Peaks ship is not an isolated incident but part of a pattern. If you use this theory, Kaelen's people have ruins in the deep desert that are structurally similar to the crashed ship — alien architecture half-buried in sand, still occasionally active, still occasionally dangerous.

### How This Plays Out

Kaelen introduces this thread naturally, without exposition dumps. She does it through observation. When the party is in the garden level and sees the ship's hydroponic systems — the artificial life being sustained by alien technology — she will say something like:

> *"This is what the ruins in the deep desert look like. Not the outside. The inside. The bones of what was keeping something alive long after the living thing was gone."*

The party will need to ask questions to get the full picture. Kaelen does not volunteer history; she answers it. This keeps the revelation organic and player-driven.

**Key Information Kaelen Can Share (if asked):**

- The Aerun's ruins predate Greenfjord by centuries. The oldest ones are not human-built.
- The Sandwalkers have a word — *vel-keth* — that translates roughly as "the hunger that eats the world." It is what they call the force that turned their homeland into a desert. Kaelen recognizes *vel-keth* in the ship's infection.
- The primal spirits of the land are not dead in the equatorial desert. They are silent. There is a difference. Dead things do not scream. Silent things are waiting.
- The ship's force fields, the infection's spread, the way it networks through living things — Kaelen has seen this pattern in the ruins. She does not know what it is called. She knows what it does.

**The Stakes:** If the party fails to neutralize the ship's systems, the infection will not merely spread through the Barrier Peaks. It will drain the living energy of the surrounding land, accelerating the process that destroyed the Aerun. The mountains will die. Then the forests. Then the plains. The world has been through this before. It barely survived.

### Mechanical Hook

Kaelen can perform a **Primal Rite of Remembrance** — a ritual that takes one hour and requires a piece of material from the ship — that allows the party to experience a vision of the Aerun as it was during the Green Age. This vision is not just atmospheric; it contains a specific piece of information about the ship's original purpose or the nature of the infection that cannot be obtained any other way. The DM should prepare this vision in advance, tailoring it to whatever revelation would be most useful at the point in the campaign when it occurs.

## Plot Thread 2: The Landsraad of the Aerun City-States

### The Political Landscape

Jak Bjornsson is not universally beloved. He is respected, feared, and in some quarters genuinely revered — but the noble and merchant houses of the major cities have watched his rise with the careful attention of people who understand what it means when one person controls the flow of information across a continent.

The Aura network is not just a communication tool. It is a surveillance apparatus. Every Spásistren operative, every Aura-implanted official, every connected node in the network feeds data back to Jak's central system. He can see trade routes, troop movements, private conversations, and political meetings — provided they happen within range of a connected node.

The noble houses have been very careful about where they hold their meetings.

**The Alliance in Formation:** Three to five of the major merchant and noble houses (the exact number and names are **[ANDY LORE]** — use whatever houses or factions have been established in the campaign) have begun meeting in the blind spots of the network. They are not yet coordinated enough to be called a Landsraad, but they are moving in that direction. They share a common grievance: Jak's monopoly on the Aura network gives him an economic and military advantage that no individual house can counter alone.

Their model is, consciously or not, the ancient Landsraad of legend — a council of houses that collectively constrain the power of a single ruler through unified economic and political pressure. They do not want to destroy Jak. They want to *negotiate* with him from a position of strength. The problem is that they have no leverage.

Until now.

### The Barrier Peaks as Political Capital

The crashed ship is the most significant technological discovery in living memory. Whoever controls access to its systems — its weapons, its medical technology, its force-field generators, its data archives — controls the most powerful bargaining chip in the known world.

Jak knows this. It is why he sent George Decay and Erleena Riser. It is why he sent the party. He wants the ship's secrets for himself, not because he is evil, but because he genuinely believes he is the right person to steward them. He is probably not wrong about this. He is also probably not the only one who should have access to them.

The noble alliance wants a representative inside the ship. They want to know what the party has found. And they want to make the party an offer.

### The Alliance's Agent

**Tomas Vel-Maret** is a merchant factor — a mid-level representative of one of the noble houses — who has been in the Barrier Peaks region for weeks, ostensibly conducting a survey of mining rights. He is, in fact, the alliance's eyes and ears in the north.

He is not a villain. He is a pragmatist who genuinely believes that Jak's monopoly on the Aura network will end badly for everyone, including Jak. He has watched what happens when a single ruler controls the flow of information across a continent. He has read the histories. He knows how those stories end.

He approaches the party after their return from the ship — perhaps at Frostwatch, or during the council scene — with a private offer: share what you have found, and the alliance will ensure that the ship's technology is distributed rather than monopolized. They are not asking the party to betray Jak. They are asking the party to consider whether Jak having exclusive access to alien weapons technology is good for the world.

**Tomas's Offer:**
- Political protection for the party in the Aerun city-states, independent of Jak's favor
- Access to the alliance's resources (information networks, safe houses, financial support)
- A promise that any technology recovered from the ship will be shared with multiple houses rather than held by a single administrator
- Specific intelligence about what Jak's Spásistren are planning to do with the ship once the party's mission is complete (the answer is: seal it, or destroy it, depending on what they find)

**Tomas's Leverage:** He knows something the party does not. The Spásistren's orders, once the party's mission is complete, are not to preserve the ship. They are to ensure that whatever the party found cannot be used by anyone else. High Sister Valerius's "three paths" council was not entirely honest. There is a fourth path that was never presented: **the ship gets sealed, the party gets rewarded, and Jak gets exclusive access to whatever data was extracted before the sealing**. Tomas has a copy of the sealed orders.

**DM Note:** Tomas is not entirely trustworthy either. The noble alliance has its own interests, and "distributed technology" means "distributed among the noble houses," not distributed to the common people. The party is being offered a choice between two forms of concentrated power. This is the Dune parallel at its most direct: the Landsraad did not oppose the Emperor because they loved freedom. They opposed him because they wanted his power for themselves. The party must decide what they believe, and who they trust.

### The Spásistren as Sardaukar

The Spásistren are Jak's Sardaukar — an elite force whose loyalty is absolute and whose effectiveness is terrifying. Like the Sardaukar, their power comes not just from training but from belief. They do not fight for pay. They fight because they believe Jak is the center of the world's order.

This makes them nearly impossible to corrupt or subvert through conventional means. The noble alliance has tried. It has not worked. The only thing that has ever given a Spásistren pause is the possibility that Jak himself might be wrong — which is why Valerius's severing of her Aura connection in the council scene is so significant. It is the first crack in the armor.

**Plot Escalation:** As the campaign continues, the party will begin to see signs that the noble alliance is growing bolder. Tomas's visits become more urgent. The Spásistren presence in the region increases. And Jak — who can see almost everything through the network — begins to ask the party pointed questions about who they have been talking to.

The party is not being asked to choose sides. They are being asked to navigate a world where every side has legitimate grievances and dangerous ambitions, and where the alien infection in the mountains might make all of it irrelevant if it is not stopped.

## Integration: How These Threads Connect

The two plot threads are not separate. They are the same story told at different scales.

| Scale | Thread 1 (Ecological) | Thread 2 (Political) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate** | The infection is spreading through the Barrier Peaks | The noble alliance is consolidating in the blind spots of the network |
| **Regional** | The mountains are dying; the infection is draining the land | The Spásistren are moving to secure the ship's perimeter |
| **Continental** | The Aerun died this way before | Jak's monopoly on the Aura network mirrors the sorcerer-kings of old |
| **Global** | If the ship is not neutralized, the world dies | If the alliance is not managed, the world goes to war |

**The Connection Point:** Kaelen is the bridge. She carries the ecological memory of Thread 1 and, as a person who exists entirely outside Jak's network, she is the kind of ally the noble alliance desperately wants. She is also the kind of person who would refuse to be used by either side. Her independence is her most valuable and most dangerous quality.

**The Party's Role:** The party is the only group with access to all three power centers — Jak's trust, the ship's interior, and (through Kaelen) the knowledge of what happened before. They are not just dungeon-crawlers anymore. They are the pivot point of a world in crisis.

## DM Notes: Tone and Pacing

**The Dark Sun Tone** should feel like ecological grief — the weight of knowing that the world has been here before and barely survived. Kaelen carries this tone. She is not hopeless, but she is not optimistic either. She is *realistic*, which is more unsettling than either.

**The Dune Tone** should feel like political vertigo — the realization that every person in the room has a hidden agenda, and that the most dangerous people are the ones who genuinely believe they are right. Tomas carries this tone. He is not lying to the party. He is telling them a true thing in service of a larger agenda.

**George Decay** remains the emotional anchor of the campaign. His dying is the personal stakes. The ecological collapse is the world stakes. The political fracture is the civilizational stakes. All three need to be present in every session, or the campaign loses its texture.

**Kaelen and George:** These two characters should develop a relationship over time. George is the scientific mind confronting the alien; Kaelen is the primal mind confronting the familiar. They are approaching the same crisis from opposite directions, and they will eventually arrive at the same conclusion: the ship is not just a threat. It is a message. Someone sent it here. The question is whether they sent it to warn, or to destroy.
