# Aerun

The equatorial desert continent — orange-sanded, sun-blasted, and the remnant of what was once a lush world. Vermoon's Dark Sun / Dune analogue, and the campaign's living warning of what the infection does when it wins.

## What It Is Now

Aerun is the central continent and the world's crossroads: industry, commerce, and trade — the place ships stop to resupply, reload, and offload between the other landmasses. Population clusters around the coasts in the Australian pattern; the interior is deep desert. A vast desert interior is crossed by nomadic survivor tribes — the **[Sandwalkers](/World/Factions/The-Sandwalkers)** — who preserve oral histories back to the Green Age and read the ruins like scripture.

Ruins are everywhere beneath the sand. The oldest are **not human-built**.

## Geography: The Concentric Rings

Aerun's environment grows progressively more hostile the further inland one travels, creating three distinct zones.

### The Coastal Ring and the City-States

The outer edge is a habitable coastal ring dominated by seven great city-states. These cities control the global commerce of Vermoon, connected by the **Ring Road** — a massive highway circling the continent that moves bulk goods the Aura network cannot teleport. While Jak maintains small temples in each city, the true power lies with the noble merchant houses that control the trade routes.

Each city-state is dominated by one of the seven great houses:

| City-State | Ruling House | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Balic** | House Wavir | Merchant-lord democracy; silt harbor fleet; salt and silver trade |
| **Draj** | House Tsalaxa | Theocratic warrior culture; god-king figurehead; hemp and grain exports |
| **Gulg** | House Inika | Forest city; sorcerer-queen styles herself a goddess; druidic templars |
| **Nibenay** | House Shom | Shadow King rules in mystery; half-giant legions; rare wood exports |
| **Raam** | House M'ke | Teetering on civil war after sorcerer-queen's death; armed factions vie for control |
| **Tyr** | House Vordon | The Free City; first to abolish slavery; council-governed; iron mines |
| **Urik** | House Stel | Militarized warrior-state; sorcerer-king still in power; sealed borders |

Home of the **fire city** — Aerun's expression of the world's elemental city-tradition (see [The Elemental Cities](/World/The-Elemental-Cities)).

### The Rim Wall West

A massive geological barrier separating the coastal ring from the interior. It is the final boundary of civilization. Beyond the Rim Wall, the Aura network fails completely — Jak is blind to everything that happens in the interior.

### The Tablelands and the Deep Desert

Between the Rim Wall and the true Deep Desert lie the **Tablelands** — a transitional zone of hostile terrain: stony barrens, sandy wastes, rocky badlands, scrub plains, and the **Silt Sea** (vast basins of fine powdery dust that flows like water, navigable only by psionic silt skimmers, and home to horrors that lurk beneath the surface).

The Deep Desert at the center is a realm of violent sandstorms and absolute water scarcity, entirely off the Aura grid. To the coastal cities, it is a dead zone. To the Sandwalkers, it is home.

## What It Was

See [The Green Age](/Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Green-Age): rivers, forests, cities — ended in the **Red Dragon's apocalypse** and remembered by the Sandwalkers as the coming of ***vel-keth***, "the hunger that eats the world." Their ancestors survived by moving rather than fighting what they could not stop. Somewhere in the deep desert, Sandwalker tradition holds, Aerun's **[Titan](/World/The-Titans)** still slumbers.

## The Secret Ecology: Spores, Worms, and Spice

The defining truth of Aerun — known only to the Sandwalkers and hidden from both the coastal nobles and Jak himself — is that the Deep Desert is not a natural phenomenon. It is a **quarantine zone**.

### The First Source

Buried somewhere in the Deep Desert is the "First Source" — an ancient origin point for a fungal/spore infection identical to the one now emerging from the crashed ship in the Barrier Peaks. The desertification of Aerun was the mechanism of containment. The desert did not happen to Aerun; it was *made*, at terrible cost, to keep something buried.

### The Lifecycle

The ecology of the Deep Desert is a closed loop that keeps the First Source contained:

1. **Spawn:** Spores spawn from the First Source.
2. **Mass:** Without anything organic to attach to in the barren desert, the spores gather into dense masses.
3. **Bloom:** The mass eventually explodes into a violent fungal bloom.
4. **Consumption:** The dead spores and fungal matter are consumed by the apex predators of the deep desert: the great worms.

### The Worms

The coastal cities know of smaller, juvenile worms that occasionally breach the Rim Wall — dangerous but manageable. The massive, ancient worms of the Deep Desert are considered myth by city-dwellers. To the Sandwalkers, they are revered forces of nature that maintain the balance of the world. Without the worms, the cycle breaks and the desert itself would eventually be consumed.

### The Spice and the Way

The byproduct of the worms consuming the dead spores is the **Spice**.

- **The Economy:** Spice is the most valuable resource on Vermoon, generating immense wealth for the coastal noble houses who export it globally.
- **The Effect:** Consumption of the Spice enables and amplifies psionic abilities — known as "the Will and the Way." Almost all beings on Aerun possess minor psionic wild talents from ambient Spice exposure; concentrated use unlocks true mastery.
- **The Mark:** Heavy, sustained use stains the sclera and iris a deep, uniform blue — an unmistakable sign of a serious practitioner.

The coastal nobles believe Spice is a naturally occurring mineral or botanical resource. The fact that it is the processed byproduct of an apocalyptic fungal infection and giant desert worms is the most closely guarded secret of the Sandwalkers.

## Magic: Preservers and Defilers

Arcane magic on Aerun is tied to the life force of the planet. Wizards fall into two camps:

**Preservers** draw only enough energy from surrounding plants to cast their spells, leaving the flora to recover. Because all magic is viewed with suspicion, Preservers operate in secret cells known as the **Veiled Alliance** — a network that works quietly toward restoring Aerun's lost greenery.

**Defilers** rip the life force from the surrounding area, turning fertile soil to sterile ash and killing plant life in a visible radius. The sorcerer-kings are defilers of the highest order, combining destructive magic with powerful psionics. Their centuries of defiling are a primary reason the Tablelands are as barren as they are. Arcane magic is widely feared and often illegal in the city-states, punishable by death or enslavement.

## Society

The coastal city-states share a brutal social hierarchy:

| Tier | Group | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sorcerer-Kings** | God-like immortal rulers; masters of defiling magic and psionics |
| 2 | **Templars** | Bureaucratic enforcers; manage water distribution; granted minor magic by their king |
| 3 | **Nobility / Merchant Houses** | Control farms, water, Spice, and the Ring Road |
| 4 | **Free Citizens** | Artisans, crafters, minor merchants; freedom is tenuous |
| 5 | **Slaves** | The economic foundation; field workers, miners, gladiators |

In cities where the sorcerer-king has fallen — Tyr, Raam, Balic, Draj — this hierarchy is in violent flux. **Gladiatorial arenas** in nearly every city-state serve as entertainment and social control; a successful gladiator is one of the only paths of upward mobility available to the enslaved.

### The Races of Aerun

- **Humans:** Dominant in the city-states; adaptable and ruthlessly ambitious.
- **Elves:** Nomadic desert runners; viewed with suspicion in cities as untrustworthy merchants and thieves — a reputation largely earned.
- **Dwarves:** Hairless and intensely focused on a single life-goal ("focus"); unmatched builders and laborers.
- **Muls:** A sterile human-dwarf crossbreed, bred for the arenas and heavy labor; human ambition combined with dwarven stamina.
- **Thri-Kreen:** Giant mantis-like insectoids who roam the deep wastes in hunting packs; perfectly adapted to the desert; rarely seen in cities except as slaves or adventuring companions.
- **Half-Giants:** Massive shock troops employed by the sorcerer-kings; emotionally volatile and easily influenced.

## Political Dynamics

The political landscape of Vermoon is defined by the tension between two absolute monopolies.

**Jak and the Aura Network** — Jak controls teleportation and instant global communication. He sees and hears everything on the grid. Beyond the Rim Wall, he is blind.

**The Noble Alliance** — The seven merchant houses control the Ring Road, the Spice trade, and the bulk shipping that keeps every city alive. They are quietly forming a Landsraad-style alliance against Jak's growing authoritarian control. See [The Noble Alliance](/Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Noble-Alliance) and [The Noble Alliance Houses](/World/Factions/The-Noble-Alliance-Houses).

The recent fall of sorcerer-kings in Tyr, Raam, Balic, and Draj has dramatically shifted this balance. The surviving sorcerer-kings of Urik, Gulg, and Nibenay are consolidating power while the leaderless cities have become arenas of factional conflict.

## Why It Matters Now

Three months ago Aerun's primal spirits began going **silent** — the Defiling Silence — resonating with the ship's activity across the sea. [Kaelen al-Hajra](/NPCs/Kaelen-al-Hajra) followed the silence north. Her message to the Council of Frostwatch: *"What is happening to your mountains — this is how it began for us. We did not stop it in time."*

## The Barrier Peaks Connection

The crashed ship in the Scarlands is a **second spore source**. Because it crashed in the cold, wet, organic-rich Scarlands mountains:

1. The spores have abundant life to attach to immediately — they do not need to mass before blooming.
2. There are no desert worms in the Scarlands to consume the blooms.

Without the worms to close the lifecycle, the infection will spread unchecked. Kaelen recognizes it immediately. The only thing keeping Aerun from being consumed is the brutal ecology of the worms and the desert — an ecology the Scarlands does not have. The party's actions in the Barrier Peaks are not merely a local adventure. They are, unknowingly, fighting the same war Aerun has been fighting for millennia.

## Using Aerun at the Table

- The party has been here before: the previous campaign touched Aerun by flight and teleporter (never by sea). The [teleport network](/World/The-Elemental-Cities) still nominally reaches it.
- Source inspiration: the Dark Sun 4e books and Modiphius Dune 2d20 line in `/assets` — see [Sourcebooks & Inspiration](/Reference/Sourcebooks-and-Inspiration).
- A future arc (Sandwalker guides, the fire city's trade politics, the sleeping Titan, Landsraad maneuvering in the city-states) is fully seeded and waiting.
- Tone: **ecological grief** — not hopeless, not optimistic; realistic, which is more unsettling than either.
