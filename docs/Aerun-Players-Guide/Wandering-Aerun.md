# Wandering Aerun

*An unlicensed voice on the Guild's own Relay, and why everyone on the Ring Road has an opinion about it.*

## What everyone knows (no check)

- Somewhere on **Relay channel forty-three** there was a broadcast that should not exist: an unlicensed program hosted by a man who calls himself only **the Wanderer**, transmitting from an undisclosed relay somewhere in the [Sea of Silt](Gazetteer/The-Sea-of-Silt.md). He talked about the roads, the gates, the water, and the people who work them, in no hurry at all.
- The Relay belongs to the Guild, only Navigators are taught its working, and no one else is supposed to be able to speak on it. **The Guild does not discuss the broadcast** — which, on Aerun, is how everyone knows it is real.
- **Transcription wafers** of past episodes circulate in the markets, and can be found tucked into copies of *A Wanderer's Journal of Aerun*. Owning one is not illegal. Nothing about the broadcast is illegal, exactly, which is part of what makes the Guild's silence so loud.

## The season, and how it ended

Eight broadcasts, and then a goodbye. On the final one the Wanderer announced, in the same unhurried voice he used for gate procedure, that he was traveling **north past First Mark and then west into the unstaked blank** — the gray no lane has ever crossed. That last broadcast came not from his hidden relay but from **Midreach's own tower**; his words on the matter were that *"a courtesy was extended,"* and he said no more about it. Since then, channel forty-three has been silent. The Guild has no comment — which, on Aerun, is how everyone knows it matters.

## The night at the Cochlea

The season's one piece of news the Wanderer covered as it happened. After **six tendays** of the porters' work-to-rule slowdown at the [Balic](Gazetteer/Balic.md) portage, the factors hired the **[Salt View](Gazetteer/Salt-View.md) Principal Troupe** to perform at the foot of the Cochlea. The troupe staged the **Fall of Yaramuke** — and all five [Great Schools](The-Great-Schools.md) walked the turns during the play: a Mender treating stair-hands free of charge, a Reckoner reading the city's rate card aloud, Kenaz, the Academy. Seven porters left the stair that night in School cloaks — the Schools bought their contracts from the city's ledger and burned the paper on the landing. The next morning the gangs ended the slowdown on their own count, and the city posted a water increase and called it "seasonal." Nobody was bought; nobody was freed beyond the seven. The Wanderer read the whole evening out on the air and declined to say what he thought it meant, which Balic took as an opinion.

## The correspondent

Through the season the Wanderer read letters on the air from an **anonymous correspondent** — an excellent hand, and a knowledge of the lanes, the ledgers, and the towers that no listener should have. The final letter was waiting for him at Midreach. It spoke of lane law's rule — *stop for nothing that hails you from the gray* — and it said:

> *"The fourth one is ours. We wrote it."*

The Wanderer read it, thanked the correspondent as he always did, and moved on to the weather. Nobody has claimed the hand. The wafers of that episode trade above the usual rate.

Caravan crews within reach of a far-speaker marked the broadcast hours the way they mark the beacon schedule. The Houses would very much like to know where the Wanderer's relay stands. So far the silt is keeping it — and now it is keeping him.
