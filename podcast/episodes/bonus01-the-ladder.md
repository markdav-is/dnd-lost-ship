## scene: welcome [radio]

INTERN: [quiet, close, a little unsure] Okay. The light's on, so I think this is going out. [beat] This is channel forty-three. I'm not the Wanderer. I mind his set while he's away. He calls me his intern — it's a northern word, and near as I can tell it means apprentice, minus the wages.

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: He's not back. I haven't heard from him. This radio's been silent since Midreach, same as yours, and it turns out a silent radio is a hard thing to sit beside every night. [beat] He left me a list of things not to touch. It's a long list. [deadpan] Talking on the air isn't on it. I looked very carefully.

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: So this isn't a show. Think of it as a letter, from out here at the edge, to whoever still has the dial on forty-three. [beat] Mostly it's for the ones my age. Half the mail that reaches this set, the half he never read out, asks one question: which School? I turned nineteen this year. I've been reading those letters by lamplight for a month, and nobody's answering them. So I will. What's good about each, what the catch is, and what your life looks like after. Then something I've been chewing on, about this continent and the rest of the world.

## scene: the-ladder [radio]

PAUSE: 1.0

INTERN: Quick ground rules, for anyone listening from off-continent. Aerun has no gods and no priests. Where other lands raise temples, we raise schools. Five of them. They train the mind — the Will and the Way — and they answer to no House, which every House quietly resents. [beat] And here's the part that matters if you're young and broke: any child who shows the Will can be tested. Freeborn, pit-born, makes no difference. The pits send their promising kids up on the masters' own coin. It's the one ladder on Aerun that reaches all the way down. [dry] Everything else here already belongs to somebody. So. Five doors. Let's knock.

## scene: stillwater [radio]

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: Stillwater. Nibenay, with a smaller hall in Raam. They take anyone who can be still — no ranks, no titles, the name is the only one you get. What you learn is how to tell when someone's lying, and how to say "stop" in a voice that makes people actually do it. [impressed] That's real, by the way. Ask anybody who's tried to haggle with one.

PAUSE: 0.8

INTERN: The career: you get placed with a House, on contract. You sit at the swearings in the white hours and nobody at the table shades the truth, because you're looking at them. Good water, good rooms, every hall on the continent lets you in. [beat] The catch is the word "placed." You're on loan. Stillwater can call you home whenever it likes, and the family you've served for ten years was never yours. [deadpan] Also, nobody can lie to you. Picture your last family dinner, and tell me that's a gift.

## scene: reckoning [radio]

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: The School of Reckoning — Draj, with an annex in Balic. These are the calculators. Not mind-readers, they'd want me to be clear on that. They just finish counting before you've started. A Master Reckoner once audited my boss on this very frequency, over a tea bill. [amused] He did not win.

PAUSE: 0.8

INTERN: The upside is the steadiest work on Aerun. Every House keeps a Reckoner, every contract signed at noon has one standing behind it, and because the School trains the body along with the mind, a Reckoner walks through any city gate and loses nothing. There's nothing to confiscate. [beat] The downside: a Reckoner dismissed from one House may never serve a rival. So your first job is your only job. [dry] Officially. Everybody knows that rule, and Draj runs on people breaking it. If you love numbers and you can keep a secret for forty years, this is your door. If you can only manage one of those, it really isn't.

## scene: kenaz [radio]

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: Kenaz, in Tyr. The best blades on Aerun, and they don't carry swords. The blade comes out of your own mind, lasts as long as your hand stays closed, and passes every gate there is. Their duels go to first touch and leave no mark, which is why the Houses hire Kenaz-sworn to settle the small arguments. Somebody wins, nobody bleeds, lunch is served.

PAUSE: 0.8

INTERN: After the school, you're a blade for hire. Every House pays, and Balic pays most. Here's what the recruiters skip: no armor. Ever. Not hide, not chitin, not a padded vest. Kenaz teaches that your protection is a discipline you hold in your head — they call it the Crag — and a mind that can't be shaken is supposed to move the body out of the way in time. [dry] Which sounds wonderful until a half-giant swings a bone club at you. You're hired, not kept — the work ends when the argument does. And choose your hall with care. Urik runs its own Kenaz, harder and narrower, for the Lion's legion-captains, and the two branches haven't spoken in a generation. [beat] Several of the Masters fight blind. I used to think that was a boast. [deadpan] I now think it's a note about the curriculum.

## scene: menders [radio]

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: The Menders' Hall. Balic, with an annex inside the living hedge at Gulg. The healers. They'll treat a slave and a merchant-king in the same room. You'll know a Mender by the Seam — a blue stitch-line tattooed straight across the forehead. Dye, not Spice, and on this continent everyone can tell the difference.

PAUSE: 0.8

INTERN: The good: you are welcome everywhere, for life. The Guild carries Menders around the whole Ring Road for nothing, and the argosy crews count one aboard as luck. You'll never go hungry, and you'll never lie awake wondering if your work mattered. [beat] The price is the Oath. You can't willingly harm anyone who hasn't harmed you first, and the Seam announces that to every stranger before you've opened your mouth. Break it, and the Hall doesn't ink over the mark. They cut it out. [pause] Oh — and the scalpel is your own forearm bone. The Hall teaches your arm to put out a blade. [deadpan] They don't bring that up at the testing. I'd have opened with it.

## scene: academy [radio]

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: [carefully] The Navigation Academy. It sits above Raam, but it isn't Raam's — it belongs to the Caravan Guild. These are the hooded ones at the bow of every silt ship and aboard every great argosy. Under a Master Navigator the Long Reach takes four days instead of six, and nobody on board can say which day went missing.

PAUSE: 0.8

INTERN: In its favor: you see the entire continent, you command the train, and you never think about money again. Against: you don't apply. The Guild chooses children, pays the family, and doesn't explain. Your eyes go blue, because nobody on Aerun takes more Spice. And as for the career after leaving... [beat] nobody's left. The Guild doesn't release its graduates. So I can't tell you what that life looks like, and I'm not going to guess out loud, because I happen to be speaking on a Guild radio and I'd like that to continue. [brightly] The Academy is wonderful. Moving on.

## scene: the-wider-world [radio]

PAUSE: 2.0

INTERN: Okay. The other thing. [beat] I grew up in a port town — I won't say which. Every morning of my childhood there were ships at anchor from places I couldn't pronounce. Northern hulls riding low with steel. Southern hulls full of timber and dye. Crews who pray — out loud, to gods, with names. They'd come down the plank wearing mail, in our sun, and you'd watch the gate clerks lift the metal off them piece by piece while they turned the color of a boiled crab.

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: Here's what gets me. Every chart in the world has Aerun in the middle. The winds won't let anyone sail past us, so nobody does — they sail to us, the cargo crosses by road, and it ships out the far side. Every bolt of cloth on the ocean is either coming here or leaving. We're the hub of the whole wheel. [beat] And almost nobody born here ever goes out along a spoke. The goods do. We weigh them, we seal them in wax, and we wave.

PAUSE: 0.9

INTERN: So when I look at those five doors, I ask a question the Schools don't paint on the wall. Does it travel? [thoughtful] A Reckoner's arithmetic works anywhere there's a ledger. A Kenaz blade would pass a northern gate as easily as one of ours — though up there everybody wears steel, and you've never owned so much as a leather vest. A Mender, in a land with temples... they'd take one look at the healing and ask which god. And the answer is "none, I went to school," and I would pay good ceramic to watch that conversation. [beat] A Navigator doesn't get to find out.

PAUSE: 1.4

INTERN: [quieter] On the coast they say that beyond the Rim Wall, the world ends. I'm sitting beyond the Rim Wall right now, and I can report that it keeps right on. It's gray, and it's hot, and there's a great deal of it. So I've stopped trusting that proverb in the other direction too. The sea isn't the edge. It's just the part we've never shipped ourselves across. [pause] My boss walked this whole continent, wrote all of it down, and then went looking for the one piece that wasn't on the chart. That stays with me, minding his radio. Whichever door you pick, make it the one that lets you walk somewhere afterward. [beat] That's my whole advice. I'm nineteen. Weigh it accordingly.

## scene: outro [radio]

PAUSE: 1.4

[pause] For everyone else — he always closed with the rules. They're his, so I'll only borrow the last. Stop for nothing that hails you from the gray. [warmly] Good night from channel forty-three. [beat] I was never here.
