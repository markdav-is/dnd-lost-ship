## scene: theme-in [dry]

MUSIC: dusty desert-blues radio show theme, twangy plucked oud and lap steel guitar over a loping hand drum, warm and inviting with a sly grin, old-time radio opener, instrumental, ends on a held note | 25

## scene: welcome [dry]

WANDERER: [warmly] Good white hours, friends. Good white hours, wherever the gray finds you — you're on Wandering Aerun, coming to you live, and I do mean live, on channel forty-three, from an undisclosed relay somewhere out in the Sea of Silt. And no — [chuckles] no, I'm not telling you where. The Guild knows this radio's missing. The Guild can keep guessing. That's half the fun.

WANDERER: If you're new — and somebody always is — I'm your guide. Call me the Wanderer, everybody does. I walk, I write things down, and then I come here and tell you about it. Now, everything on this show is what a well-traveled man believes to be true, which... [dry] look, it's not quite the same thing as true, I'll grant you. But it beats anything the Houses are selling.

WANDERER: So — tonight. Tonight I'm excited, because we're finally doing the Long Reach. The big one. The Guild lane straight across the open silt, Raam Shelf to Balic Crest — three hundred and fifty miles of, and I want to be precise here, absolutely nothing. And it's the best road on the continent! It's the only road where the shortcut is the safe way, and — here's my favorite part — nobody can tell you who built it. Nobody! [delighted] Marvelous. Anyway — I've got the keeper of Midreach on the band, I've got mail, I've got notices. Pour the tea. Here we go.

## scene: silt-report [radio]

WANDERER: First up, like always — the silt report. Courtesy of the Guild's beacon log, and one keeper who owes me a favor. [rustle of paper] Right. Winds out of the west. [beat] The wind is always out of the west, friends. Always. The day it isn't? You won't need me to tell you. Temperatures — one twenty-two on the Balic Crest, one thirty-one in the shade at Midreach... and get this, out at the harvest sidings yesterday it touched one fifty. One fifty! [deadpan] That's not weather anymore. That's a verdict. Moving on. Dust ceiling's high, visibility's good the whole length of the Reach — which sounds like good news until an old hand reminds you that everything can see you too. And they will remind you. At length. Now — markers seventy through ninety are leaning after last week's blow. The Guild says the re-staking crews are out. The crews say — and I love this — the crews say the Guild's welcome to come hold the mekillot rib themselves while something watches from the shallows. [sarcastic] And honestly? Both sides have a point. What else... harvest sidings, open. Balic portage — backed up two days at the Cochlea. Porters' strike. [scoffs] Third one this season, and you know what? My advice hasn't changed: pay the porters. Pay. The porters. That's the report. Stay between the markers.

## scene: sponsors-1 [radio]

ANNOUNCER: Support for Wandering Aerun comes from the School of Reckoning, Draj. A Reckoner counts your coin before you have opened the purse. The School of Reckoning: we already know.

ANNOUNCER: And from the Menders' Hall of Balic. We'll patch back together what the desert has broken. The Menders' Hall — look for the Seam, and count the stitches.

ANNOUNCER: And from the city of Tyr. The Iron City. Home of the continent's only iron mines — a fact Tyr never stops mentioning. Tyr: we killed our champion so you don't have to.

## scene: interview [radio]

WANDERER: All right, all right — my guest tonight. Five days out in the middle of the gray, a man whose tea I have drunk considerably more of than I've paid for — Bellin! Keeper of Midreach waystation. Bellin, you there?

KEEPER: [easy swagger] Loud and clear, Wanderer, loud and clear. And hey — ol' Bellin doesn't forget a ledger. You still owe for the tea.

WANDERER: [laughs] I know, I know — it's on the ledger, I promise. Bellin, do me a favor. New listeners tonight. Paint them the picture — what is Midreach?

KEEPER: What is Midreach! [chuckles] Okay. Friend — Midreach is the center of the known world. Now, granted, the known world out here is two and a half days of gray in every direction — but that's the thing, out here? It is. Water, beds, weather. And the last honest cup of tea between you and Balic. Poured by the last honest man. [beat] That's me, by the way.

WANDERER: [laughing] Yes, I gathered. And the season? I keep hearing the crews are running heavy.

KEEPER: Heavy and cheerful. Which — and write this down — ol' Bellin does not trust. War freight's paying triple right now, so everybody's smiling, everybody's flush. Common room's full every single night. Oh — you'll like this — had a Salt View troupe come through last week, right? They get up and do the whole second act of the Fall of Yaramuke. On my porch! Made two crews cry. [dry] Charged them for it, obviously.

WANDERER: [delighted] Obviously! The Code's the Code. Okay but Bellin — Bellin. I have to ask. Three separate crews have written me about this. Three. They say ships keep putting in at Midreach and leaving lighter than they arrived. So — what are they leaving with you?

KEEPER: [pause] Nothing that was declared.

WANDERER: [amused] Bellin. Come on. That is a waystation keeper's answer and you know it.

KEEPER: [grinning] And it's the one you're getting, partner. Tell you what — you want more? Come out and audit me yourself. Bring what you owe. Ol' Bellin's books balance. [beat] It's the desert that doesn't.

WANDERER: [warmly] Ha! There it is. Midreach waystation, friends — the water's neutral ground, the tea's worth the markup, and the keeper is exactly like that in person. Bellin — always a pleasure.

KEEPER: [warm swagger] Anytime, friend, anytime. Mind the markers going home. And hey — [beat] send the money. Ol' Bellin always collects.

## scene: mail-run [radio]

WANDERER: Okay — mail run. [rustle of paper] First one. This is from a drover on the Ring Road, and I'm going to read it exactly as written, because it deserves that. Quote: "Wanderer. My Navigator hasn't spoken in six days. Is that normal?" [beat] [deadpan] Friend. Yes. Yes, and let me go further — that's the good outcome. A quiet Navigator is a working Navigator. The day yours starts talking? [flat] Write me again. And write your family first.

WANDERER: Second letter. No signature. [dry] Lovely penmanship, though — someone had tutors. It says, quote, the Long Reach never bends northwest, and I — quote — "need not speculate as to why." [beat] [sarcastic] Well. I hadn't been speculating. I want that on the record — hadn't crossed my mind. I am now speculating a great deal. So, to my anonymous correspondent: you clearly know the channel. Come on the show. [dry chuckle] We'll speculate together. Bring the good ink.

## scene: notices [radio]

WANDERER: Community notices — here we go. Lost: one kank. Answers to Bitey. Last seen at the Altaruk gate wearing, and I quote, half a harness and an attitude. Reward in ceramic, no questions asked — [dry] which, at Altaruk, goes without saying. Next — the Menders' clinic at First Mark is open through the white hours, all tenday. Line forms early. The Seam is real, count the stitches, you know the drill. And — oh, this is my favorite thing all week — Salt View's troupe is holding auditions for the spring circuit. They are looking for, and I am quoting the actual notice here: "two heroes, a tyrant, and someone comfortable being eaten nightly." [beat] [deadpan] Friends. That's just Aerun. That is the entire continent in one job posting. Somebody frame it.

## scene: outro [radio]

WANDERER: And... that's the hour, friends, that's the hour. Thank you to Bellin — seriously, pay your keepers, they notice, they keep ledgers — and thank you, as ever, to whoever keeps this old radio humming. [warmly] You know I don't ask. Next time on Wandering Aerun — oh, it's a good one — the white hours. Why does the entire continent stop at noon? What does the smart money do while everything's stopped? I'm trying to get a Reckoner on the band to tell us what the smart money actually is. [beat] [dry] If he'll say. They always know, friends. They never say.

WANDERER: Until then — the old rules. The only four worth keeping, say them with me: pay the rate. Answer the beacon. Drink your water in the shade. And stop for nothing — nothing — that hails you from the gray. [beat, softer] That last one's not a saying, friends. That one's the law. [warmly] Good night from channel forty-three.
