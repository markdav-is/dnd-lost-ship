# The Merchant Alliance

The seven merchant houses of Aerun's city-states are coalescing in the Aura network's blind spots into something that is not yet — but wants to be — a Landsraad.

## The standoff of monopolies

Jak's monopoly on the [Aura network](/World/The-Aura-Network) — teleportation and instant communication — meets its counterweight: the houses control the **Spice** (which the empire needs for psionics and long life) and the **Ring Road** bulk commerce that teleporters cannot carry. Neither monopoly can crush the other; both are looking for the tiebreaker. The crashed ship — the most significant technological discovery in living memory — is exactly that. Whoever controls its weapons, medicine, force fields, and archives holds the most powerful bargaining chip in the world.

Full faction detail: [The Merchant Houses](/World/Factions/The-Merchant-Houses).

## The agent: Tomas Vel-Maret

A merchant factor conducting a "mining rights survey" in the Barrier Peaks — actually the houses' eyes in the north. See [his NPC page](/NPCs/Tomas-Vel-Maret) for the offer he makes the party and the sealed orders he holds.

## DM guidance

- The alliance is **not the good guys**. "Distributed technology" means distributed among the houses. The party is being offered a choice between two forms of concentrated power — the Dune parallel at its most direct.
- The houses believe the Spice is natural. **None of them know** what it really is ([The First Source](/Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source)) — a truth that would detonate their whole economy.
- Which houses lead the alliance (Wavir's principle? Tsalaxa's paranoia? Vordon's ambition?) is the DM's choice — rosters and temperaments are on the [faction page](/World/Factions/The-Merchant-Houses).
- The Spásistren cannot be corrupted by the houses; belief is their armor. The only crack is doubt in Jak himself — which is why [Valerius's severing](/NPCs/High-Sister-Valerius) matters politically as well as spiritually.

## Escalation

Tomas's visits grow urgent → Spásistren presence increases → Jak starts asking the party who they've been talking to. The party isn't asked to pick a side; they're asked to navigate a world where every side has legitimate grievances and dangerous ambitions — while the infection threatens to make all of it irrelevant.
