A wide 16:9 painting in exactly the same painted style, colours and lighting as the reference images. The lighthouse must be the same tower, unchanged: white concrete wrapped in pale glowing root-like mycelium cords and fungal shelves, the ragged breach halfway up spilling cords, the lit lantern room with its railing, the open door at the base glowing orange, and the enormous golden-white galaxy of spores spiralling around the lantern room.

Camera: standing on the ground at the foot of the tower, close to it, looking UP at it from below — a dramatic low-angle shot, the tower soaring up into the frame, slightly right of centre, its top with the lantern room and the spore galaxy high in the upper part of the frame. We are on the island itself: there is NO lake, NO water, NO bridge and NO shoreline anywhere in the picture. The ground in the lower part of the frame is the island's inner garden: spongy russet-gold mold and pale moss skinned over cracked old concrete, thick ropy mycelium roots spreading out from the tower's base, small glowing fungus, orange spore haze rolling low across it.

The lighthouse beam: a single long, soft-edged searchlight beam of pale gold light shoots straight out HORIZONTALLY from the lantern room to the left, level with the lantern, reaching far away into the dark distance and dissolving into the murk, full of glittering spores. It does not point down at the ground. Above and around, the dark storm-like murk of the original, lit from within by the spiral.

In the moss in the lower foreground, small: five or six soft, fat, rust-coloured rabbits flecked with gold spores, sitting still, facing the tower. Cute, not monstrous.

No people. No text. Awe-inspiring, sacred, vast, beautiful and quietly wrong.
