# The Gate Ritual

*What happens at your first city gate on Aerun — the declaration, the scale, the wax, and the port where everything waits for you.*

**Player-facing and spoiler-free.** Every traveler goes through this once, at whichever gate they first present themselves to; every gate after that only checks the registry. Read the [Primer](Aerun-A-Primer.md) first for why the continent works this way.

## The declaration

At your **first gate on the continent** — and only the first — you are asked *"What's your business?"*, and the clerk means all of it. You declare five things:

1. **Your business** — why you are on Aerun.
2. **Your goods** — what you carry to sell.
3. **Your metals** — every ounce, down to the buckles.
4. **Your material components** — everything a spell could be built from.
5. **Your exit city** — the port you will eventually leave Aerun by.

The fifth is the one travelers get wrong, and the one that matters most.

## The components

Material components are **confiscated, sealed in wax, and logged in triplicate** — one copy for the gate, one for the Guild, one pressed into your hand. Then they are carried under **Guild bond** to your declared exit city, where they wait for you. A season, a year, however long your business takes: they wait. You travel Aerun without your magic, and you collect it on your way off the continent.

This is not theft, and an Aerunite will be offended if you call it that. The Merchant's Code is exact: cargo bonded is returned. It is simply returned *at the far end*.

## The metal

All metal — weapons, armor, buckles — is likewise taken at the scale, logged, and carried to your exit city, to be returned on departure. In exchange, **the gate fills your water skins: water for metal, weight for weight**, at a rate posted daily beside the scale.

Northerners call this robbery until they have walked a tenday of the Ring Road. Out here the metal was always the cook-pot, and the water was always the treasure.

Gold need not travel to the exit city at all: it may instead be **exchanged at the gate table for local currency** — ceramic, bone, black glass — at rates set by the Reckoners.

## The bond follows the declaration

Understand this before you speak at the table: **the bond follows the declaration, not the traveler.** Your goods wait at the city you *named*, whether or not you ever arrive there. Change your plans mid-journey and your sword and your spell pouch will sit in a Guild vault on the wrong coast, faithfully, forever. So declare the port you mean.

Amendments are possible — a Guild factor, a fee, a wait while the registry letters cross the continent — but the road is long and the fee is not small. Cheaper to know your route before you know your first gate.

## What it comes to

> Nothing is taken. Everything is held. You leave the continent exactly as armed as you came to it.

Lie at the table, and the registry remembers — in every city, because it is one registry. Cast openly inside a city with components you failed to declare, and no writ will save you.
