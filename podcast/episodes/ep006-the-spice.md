## scene: the-blue [radio]

WANDERER: So. [rustle of paper, then the paper set down] The Spice. Let me tell you what a well-traveled man believes to be true, and for once, friends, I'll be careful with every word of it. It's a grain — and here's the first thing every new listener gets wrong: it isn't blue. It's a dull orange. Rust, like old iron ground fine. The blue is what it leaves. Use it long enough and the eyes go blue within blue, and on this continent that stain is the only rank everyone salutes — a beggar with blue eyes is a story walking, and nobody interrupts a story. It comes off the silt — the harvest sidings, the shoals, places with names the crews don't use twice — and it comes off in castings, and what that word means is a question I have stopped asking. It lengthens life. That's not rumor; you've seen the proof walking. It strengthens the Will — what the Schools train for years, the rust hands over early and asks payment for later.

PAUSE: 0.9

WANDERER: Who takes it? The Navigators, more than anyone alive — that's not gossip, that's arithmetic; nobody signs for what the Guild consumes. The desert people, who were born closer to it than the rest of us will ever get. And Aerunites, friends. Us. I want to say this plainly, because I've watched outlanders come off the ships with coin in hand and a plan: the blue is ours. It grew out of this ground and it grows into the people of this ground, and every travelers' tale I have ever collected about an outlander who took it ends the same three ways — sick, broke, or gone. The gates don't ban it. They don't need to. The Spice knows its own. [beat] Take that as superstition if you like. Take it as a well-traveled man counting outcomes.

PAUSE: 0.9

WANDERER: And the price. The board says a pinch costs what a laborer earns in a tenday, and the board is the cheap part — because castings don't jump into the sack, friends. Harvesting is dangerous, processing is worse, neither is cheap, and the Houses claw at one another for the contracts to supply the Guild, who buy everything and explain nothing. And here's the wrinkle that keeps the coastal factors up at night: the deep desert holds no territory. No charter, no writ, no flag. Where the Spice reaches the surface is anyone's guess, and the guess belongs to whoever's standing over it with a shovel. [beat] The saying goes: the Spice pays for itself. [pause] That is the problem, friends. It pays for itself the way the Guild pays for a waystation — because it intends to use you for a very long time. [quiet] Which brings us to the part I promised.

## scene: the-once [radio]

PAUSE: 2.0

WANDERER: I took it once. [pause] Years ago, in the deep desert, under advisement — a guide I trusted, a fire, a pinch under the tongue, the Navigators' way. And I'm not going to dress this up for radio, friends; you'd hear the seams. The taste is blue. That's the word every user reaches for and I reached for it too, and no one has improved on it, and now I understand why. And for one hour — my guide says it was an hour; I have no number of my own — I stood on the road of my life and I could see it running both directions. Behind me and ahead. And friends... [long pause] I could not tell which was which. That's all of it. That's the whole once. I came back to the fire, and I drank three skins of water, and I have not taken it twice — which those who know me may count either as wisdom or as fear. [quiet] Both are blue at the edges.

## scene: mail-run [radio]

WANDERER: Mail run. [rustle of paper] First — a factor in Raam writes: "Wanderer. Does the Spice improve the bookkeeping?" [beat] [dry] Friend, it improves your certainty. Which, in bookkeeping, is worse. Ask your Reckoner. Ask her twice.

PAUSE: 1.2

WANDERER: And... [pause] the second letter. [paper handled slowly] Friends, my anonymous correspondent has written back. Same hand. Same excellent ink. And I invited this, on the air, so I'm going to honor it and read the whole thing, which is not long. Quote. "You asked why the Reach never bends northwest. You already know. You have been closer than the markers — once, in the desert, for one hour. The blue remembers you." [long pause] [quietly] ...That's the letter. [pause] I read my mail on this show, friends, and I don't perform surprise, so I'll tell you honestly: I told the story of the once tonight for the first time anywhere. And this letter was handed up the Reach four days ago. [beat] We're going to sit with that, you and I. Next segment.
