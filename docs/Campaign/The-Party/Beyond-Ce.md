## Backstory

**Full text:** [`assets/beyond-ce-backstory.md`](../../../assets/beyond-ce-backstory.md) — Andy's, pasted complete on 2026-09-22 (the PDF export cut it off). She is *she* throughout. The shape of it:

- **Not a hero, and not interested.** *"Heroes seek glory. They tell stories about themselves... Beyond-Ce has always been more practical."* A paid **escort and guide**: merchants, couriers, adventurers, and people who would rather not say why they're leaving town. She doesn't ask questions and doesn't make promises she can't keep. ***"If Beyond-Ce agrees to get you somewhere, you will probably get there."* The important word is *probably*.**
- **The Road.** Traveling with people means knowing when to stop, when to run, and which one will panic first. She reads people. She doesn't enjoy fighting; it's part of the job. Quiet, professional, never raises her voice — *"something about the way she watches a situation."* **Her one rule: once she accepts responsibility for someone, she sees the job through.** It has gotten her into trouble more than once.
- **The Night in the Woods.** An ordinary escort job, third night, camp. **The fire went out — all at once.** Then the darkness *moved*: something that slid between the trees swallowing the light, not built of bones or muscle. A client ran; she went after them — *"not because they were paying her. Because they were* hers." She put herself between the thing and the traveler, drew her weapon, thought *this is going to hurt* — and **everything disappeared**. No sound, no light, no ground. For a moment she was nowhere and everywhere, could see her people and the creature both, impossibly far off — **and then something else. Something watching. The sensation of being noticed.** Then she was back, several feet from where she'd stood, nose bleeding, **darkness clinging to her hands**, the creature gone and the traveler alive. She never learned why she survived.
- **What came back with her.** Darkness stopped behaving normally around her: she sees where others can't, feels what's hiding, and shadows move when nothing moves them. Something cold stirs in her when she's frightened or angry. The first time she made darkness on purpose she thought she was losing her mind; the second time she knew she could control it. **Shadow magic.** Born with it, given it by the creature, or brought back from that place — *she has deliberately avoided finding out. For now, the magic is useful. That's enough.*
- **The Hound.** The part that bothers her most. When she reaches deep, **a great black hound** comes out of the shadows, hunts who she points it at, and vanishes when it's done. She calls it a tool. But its *eyes* are familiar — and sometimes, when she summons it, **it looks at her first, as if it recognizes her. As if it is waiting for something.** She has never told anyone that.
- **The Road Ahead.** Years on. Still a guide, for the pay, for the road — and because she's admitted something to herself: *she is more comfortable responsible for someone else than for herself alone.* A client means a destination, a job, and someone to protect. She's escorted people she disliked and taken coin from people hiding things. *Money is money.* But if she gives her word she'll get you there, she will, whatever it costs. ***"She may not be a hero. But she does not leave people behind."***

## DM hooks

- **The thing in the woods, and the watcher.** Andy wrote a creature that swallows light, a moment *outside* — nowhere and everywhere — and something that *noticed* her there. That is the character's mystery and it is **Andy's to answer**, not the wiki's. Do not tie it to the Black Salient, the Lighthouse, or Rajaat without his say-so; a sorcerer's shadow origin doesn't need to be the campaign's villain. Keep it as texture — a candle that gutters near her, a hound that looks at her a beat too long — until he decides.
- **The Hound of Ill Omen** is on her sheet as a subclass feature. Now it is also a *character*: play the look it gives her before it turns. Never explain it.
- **Her rule and the party.** *Once she accepts responsibility for someone, she sees the job through.* She has, in effect, accepted responsibility for four companions, a professor, and a box of books. That is why she's here; it doesn't need saying out loud.
- **Took heavy psychic damage and was briefly dominated** in the mind flayer theater fight — psychic scars available for callbacks.
- Faction Agent background: the natural hook for [Tomas Vel-Maret and the merchant alliance](../Plot-Threads/The-Merchant-Alliance.md) to make contact through. Which faction is the blank on the sheet.
- Player Andy is the world's creator-DM and keeper of Aerun deep lore — the remaining **[ANDY LORE]** insertion points in [The Green Age](../Plot-Threads/The-Green-Age.md) are his to define, in or out of character.
- A sorcerer watching wizards defile: if any arc tempts a player character toward the [Arcane Defilement](../../Game-Mechanics/Dark-Sun-5e-Rules.md) rules, the party's arcanist has a front-row seat — and Andy knows exactly what it costs.

- **She is an escort by trade, and the arc just handed her a client.** The Return to the Ship arc ends with the party taking [Erleena](../../NPCs/Erleena-Riser.md) — and fifty pounds of books — off the ship, across the ice, and over a sea to Aerun. That is, word for word, the job on Beyond-Ce's sheet: get someone from one place to another, through danger, and don't abandon them when it goes wrong. Let Erleena *hire* her, or try to. *"Probably"* is the word to play with.
- **Aerun runs on her profession.** A continent of caravans, bonded cargo, and guides who state a price ([Aerun — A Primer](../../Aerun-Players-Guide/Aerun-A-Primer.md)) is a place where a reliable escort with a reputation is understood immediately — and where the Transit Guild has opinions about freelancers.
