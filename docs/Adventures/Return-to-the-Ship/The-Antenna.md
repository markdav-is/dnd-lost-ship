# The Antenna

*Return to the Ship, side quest — Eddie wants a radio dish on top of the ship, pointed at the Temple of Nord, so he can join the Aura network. The party has to carry it up a mountain, hammer it into the rock, wire it up on his instructions, and aim it, while birds attack and Eddie helps. **This one is a comedy. Run it as one.***

## What Eddie wants, and why

George and Erleena woke Eddie for two things: cure research, and **getting a message out**. The second has beaten him for a month. The ship's fields filter radio at the hull ([The Lost Ship](../../The-Lost-Ship.md)) — nothing transmitted inside gets out.

The fields stop at the hull. **A wire doesn't care.** A dish mounted *outside*, on the bare top of the ship, cabled in through a service port, gives Eddie a voice the fields can't swallow. Point it at the strongest Aura node on the continent — the **Temple of Nord** in Grønnfjord — and he is on the network.

> *"It's a very small favor. You carry a dish up a hill, you stand it up, you plug it in, and you point it at your friend Jak's house. I'd do it myself but I don't have — well. Any of the parts you'd need. Legs, mostly."*

> *"I'll be honest, I don't actually know where his house is. My charts are a bit out of date. But you've been there! You can just point."*

**Why not send a robot?** Eddie doesn't drive his robots; he **gives them tasks, and they go and do them on their own**. That is how they can work outside the hull at all, where his voice doesn't reach. It's also why they're useless here: nobody ever wrote a robot a task called *carry a dish up a mountain, hammer it into a rock, and point it at a temple you've never heard of*. A robot outside can't be talked through anything. He tried one anyway. It carried the dish to the foot of the rocks, set it down very neatly, and came back in, job done.

> *"They're wonderful at lists. This isn't a list. This is more of a — a situation. You're much better at situations."*

**This counts as Eddie's favor** ([The Lab at S42](The-Lab-at-S42.md)) if the table hasn't already paid it.

## The kit

The robots — tasked with *fetch the following from stores* — stack it all by the upper airlock, and stand beside it, finished:

| Item | What it is | The problem with it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The dish** | A directional dish wider than a wagon wheel. **150 lb.** | **Nobody flies it up.** It's far past what [Eustace](../../Campaign/The-Party/Eustace-Thunderbeak.md) can lift on the wing, and in this wind it's a sail: anything airborne holding it — wings, belt, spell — gets thrown off the mountain. It goes up on somebody's back. ([Musty-Jo](../../Campaign/The-Party/Musty-Jo-Mustang.md) can carry it without noticing. He has opinions about being a pack animal; let him have them.) |
| **The tower** | A folding three-legged mast, **three iron feet**, three two-foot **spikes**, and a **sledgehammer**. 80 lb the lot. | The feet have to be pounded into the rock. With the hammer. By hand. |
| **The signal cable** | A heavy spool. One end plugs into the dish, the other into a service port under a hinged plate up top. | It's a spool. It rolls. Downhill. |
| **The speaker** | **A breadbox-sized gray metal box with a grille on the front and a carrying handle, 25 lb, trailing a cable all the way back down to the airlock.** This is Eddie. | See below. |

### The speaker

**Eddie's voice ends at the hull.** The party's phones are no help — they aren't radios; a phone only talks *through the Aura network*, and the ship isn't on the network, which is the entire reason they're going up there. So if they want Eddie's instructions — and they need Eddie's instructions — **somebody has to lug the box**, and the box is on a **wire**.

> *"You'll want to take me with you. Well — a bit of me. The talking bit. Mind the cable. There's four hundred feet of it and it's the only one I've got."*

Rules for the box:

- It takes **one hand, always.** Whoever carries it is tethered to the ship like a dog on a lead, and everyone else will trip over the lead.
- **Eddie never stops talking.** He narrates. He encourages. He asks if they're warm enough. He reads safety notices from the manual. He apologizes in advance.
- **The cable is the running gag.** It snags. It tangles around legs — especially Musty-Jo's, of which there are four. Birds take an interest in it. If it's **yanked out** at the airlock end, Eddie cuts off mid-word and someone has to go back down and plug him in (or wait 1d4 rounds for a robot to notice). If it's **cut**, it can be spliced: tinker's tools DC 12, one round, during which Eddie is heard as a series of vowels.
- **They can leave it behind.** Then they do the whole job with no instructions, which is its own kind of funny: all tool checks below at DC +3, aiming blind (see Act 4), and no warning before the turrets fire.

## Where it is

Out the **upper airlock** in the hillside dell, then **up**: three hundred feet of iced boulders to where the tremor shook the top of the hull bare — a long hump of gray metal standing out of the snow and rock. Eddie calls it **the crown**. He holds the airlock door for them. *"I'll keep the door. I'm good at doors."*

The siege is still on ([The Breach](The-Breach.md)). The ground horde boils around the lower hull; the crown is above the worst of it. **The sky is not.**

## The job, in four acts

Each act is a couple of rounds. **Every round, roll on *And Whatnot* (below).** Failures cost time and dignity, never the quest.

### Act 1 — Up the rocks

Three hundred feet of scrambling over ice-glazed boulders with a wagon wheel, a tower, a spool, and a talking breadbox.

- **Strength (Athletics) DC 14** per carrier, one check per hundred feet. Musty-Jo ignores the weight and rolls only for footing — hooves on ice, **disadvantage** — unless someone thinks to rope him.
- **Fail:** slide back 30 ft, 1d6 bludgeoning, and whatever you were carrying goes further than you did. **The spool, once dropped, rolls all the way back to the airlock**, paying out cable the entire way. A robot brings it back up to the door and waits there, holding it out, expectantly.
- The speaker carrier checks at disadvantage (one hand). Eddie counts the rocks for them.

### Act 2 — Pound in the feet

The mast stands on three iron feet, and each foot takes a spike, and each spike has to go two feet into frozen rock. There is one sledgehammer.

- **Each spike: Strength (Athletics) DC 13**, one swing a round. Three spikes. Someone has to **hold the spike** while someone else swings.
- **Fail by 5 or more:** you hit the holder. 1d8 bludgeoning and a conversation.
- **Natural 1:** the hammer head flies off and goes down the mountain. Eddie has a spare. It's at the airlock.
- Eddie has read that the correct torque is "firm." He can also hear every impact through the hull. *"Ooh. That one I felt. No, no, carry on, it's fine, it's mostly fine."*
- Holding a spike means not holding a weapon. Swinging the hammer means not holding a weapon. The birds have noticed.

### Act 3 — The blue wire

Dish on the mast, cable into the service port. Under the hinged plate is the port — and beside it a panel Eddie did not mention, full of **wires**. The dish has a transit lock, and the lock has to be bypassed before it will power up. Eddie has the manual. The manual is nine hundred years old and so are the wires.

> *"Right. Easy bit. Cut the blue wire."*
>
> *"...There are three blue wires."*
>
> *"Are there? Oh. Well, it's the bluest one."*

Three wires have to be dealt with. For each one:

1. **Eddie names a color.** Whoever has the cutters commits: **cut, or wait.** Ask fast. Don't let them think.
2. **Roll a d6 in the open. On a 1–2, Eddie changes his mind a half-second late:** *"No! The red wire! The RED wire!"* If they said *cut*, the cutter makes a **Dexterity save DC 13** to stop the snips in time.
3. If they waited and he didn't change his mind, he gently wonders aloud what the holdup is while a bird arrives. Roll *And Whatnot* an extra time.
4. **A right wire:** a lamp on the panel goes from red to amber. **A wrong wire:** roll below, then Eddie finds the correct page. *"Ah — I was reading you the instructions for the galley dishwasher. Same manufacturer. Very similar panel."*

| d4 | Wrong wire |
| :-: | :--- |
| 1 | **The dish does one full, fast rotation.** Everyone within 5 ft: DEX save DC 13 or 1d6 bludgeoning and knocked prone. Any bird perched on it is launched. |
| 2 | **Every floodlight on the hull comes on at once**, along with the marker lamps, pointed at the party. 1d4 more perytons come to see. |
| 3 | **The speaker emits a feedback squeal** that can be heard in Frostwatch. Everyone within 30 ft is deafened for a round — including the birds, which scatter for a round. Eddie didn't notice. *"Sorry, was that me?"* |
| 4 | **The ship's horn sounds.** One long, mournful blast that shakes snow off the peaks. *"That's the lunch whistle. Sorry. Nobody's been to lunch in a while."* Roll *And Whatnot* twice next round. |

Anyone with **tinker's or thieves' tools, or Intelligence (ship systems / Arcana) DC 15**, can ignore Eddie and trace a wire themselves — one round per wire, no d6. Eddie is supportive and a little hurt.

### Act 4 — Warmer, colder

Third lamp goes amber; the dish hums. Now it has to point at the Temple of Nord: **north-northwest**, a very long way off, in a blizzard. Eddie can't say where that is, but he can say **how much signal he's getting**, and does.

**Three successes before three failures**, one check a round, by whoever is on the dish:

- **Wisdom (Survival) DC 15**, or **cartographer's tools DC 13** (Musty-Jo is proficient). **Eustace, above the murk,** grants advantage by calling the line of the valley toward Frostwatch. Anyone who made the trip down from Grønnfjord can spend their turn **remembering the road** for +1d4.
- **Success:** *"Warmer! Oh, that's — yes. Left a touch. No, my left. No — you don't have a my left up there, do you. Your left."*
- **Failure:** *"Colder. Colder. That's a mountain. I'm getting a very clear picture of a mountain."*
- **Three successes:** throw the mast clamp. The lamps go green.
- **Three failures:** it comes up anyway, **intermittent** — works when the weather's kind. Eddie says it's perfect. It can be re-aimed another day.
- **No speaker: aiming blind.** Disadvantage, and don't announce results. The only feedback on the whole mountain is three little lamps: amber while it hunts, green when it locks.

## And Whatnot

Roll a d8 **every round**, all four acts. The birds are [Fungal Perytons](../../Bestiary/Fungal-Threats.md) (CR 2 — at level 11 they are a nuisance, which is the point), a rolling handful of 3–4 at any time, replaced as they drop. One [Spore-Touched Griffon](../../Bestiary/Fungal-Threats.md) turns up when the DM wants the scene to have a villain.

| d8 | What now |
| :-: | :--- |
| 1 | **A peryton grabs the speaker cable and flies off with it.** Speaker carrier: STR save DC 13 or dragged 15 ft across the rocks. Eddie's voice recedes and returns. *"— aaaand I'm back. Hello."* |
| 2 | **The cable's snagged** on a rock two hundred feet down. The box won't go another inch. Someone goes back and frees it, or yanks (STR DC 15) — fail by 5+ and it pulls out at the airlock, and Eddie stops mid-sent— |
| 3 | **Eddie provides covering fire.** Everyone on the crown: DEX save DC 13 or 2d6 radiant. *"Sorry! Sorry. That one was mine. Nearly had it."* He does kill a bird. |
| 4 | **Eddie is on the wrong page.** His next instruction is confidently for a different piece of equipment. Next tool check at disadvantage unless someone catches it (Insight DC 12: he's describing a chair). |
| 5 | **A peryton lands on the dish** and settles, as on a nest. The dish swings off true. It will not be shooed. It will be shot. Then it falls on someone. |
| 6 | **The wind takes the dish.** Whoever's holding it: STR save DC 14 or slides 20 ft and spends a round climbing back. If no one's holding it, *it* goes 20 ft. |
| 7 | **Eddie wants to chat.** Is now a good time? He's been meaning to ask what snow is *like*. Next check by whoever's nearest the box is at disadvantage — unless someone tells him to be quiet, in which case he is, completely, including for the next instruction they need. |
| 8 | **A bird steals something**: the hammer, a spike, the cutters, Eustace's hat. It doesn't want it. It drops it somewhere inconvenient. |

### The birds, at the table

**Fungal Peryton** — *Medium monstrosity, CR 2 (450 XP)*

| AC | HP | Speed | STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 13 | 33 (6d8+6) | 20 ft, **fly 60 ft** | 16 (+3) | 12 (+1) | 13 (+1) | 9 (−1) | 12 (+1) | 10 (+0) |

- **Skills** Perception +5 (passive 15) · **Resist** bludgeoning, piercing, slashing from nonmagical attacks · **Senses** keen sight and smell (advantage on Perception using them)
- **Flyby.** No opportunity attacks when it flies out of reach — it hits and keeps going, which is why it is so annoying.
- **Dive Attack.** If it dives at least 30 ft straight at a target and hits with its gore, **+2d8 damage** (9).
- **Spore Release** *(fungal)*. When it takes piercing or slashing damage, a 10-ft cloud of spores bursts out: **DC 13 CON** or poisoned for 1 minute (repeat the save at the end of each turn).
- **Multiattack:** one gore, one talons.
  - **Gore** +5 to hit, reach 5 ft: **1d8+3** piercing (7).
  - **Talons** +5 to hit, reach 5 ft: **2d4+3** piercing (8).

*In this scene:* keep 3–4 in the air, replace them as they drop, and let them go for the **cable, the hammer, and hats** before they go for throats. A level-11 party kills one a round without trying; the trouble is that everyone's hands are full.

**Spore-Touched Griffon** — *Large monstrosity, CR 3*: AC 12 · HP 59 · fly 80 ft · Beak +6 (1d8+4) · Claws +6 (2d6+4) **and the target is grappled** (escape DC 14) · **Drag-down:** hauls a grappled creature 30 ft a turn · 5-ft **spore aura**, DC 13 CON at the start of your turn in it or poisoned until the end of that turn. (Full notes on [The Breach](The-Breach.md).)

**The griffon** goes for whoever is holding the dish; its grapple-and-drag pulls them off the crown and down the slope. The dish has **AC 13, 20 HP**; at 0 it's scrap, and Eddie has exactly one spare and a much more sheepish voice.

The birds break off when the lamps go green and the turrets no longer have to shoot around the party.

## When it connects

> *The lamps go green. The wind drops, as if on cue. From the gray box, very quietly: "Oh. Oh, there's so much of it."*

Let that land for one breath. Then:

**The link is to the ship, not to the mountain.** The dish gives *Eddie* a line onto the Aura network; it does not make the ship a tower. The party's phones are exactly as dead on the crown as they were five minutes ago, and they stay dead anywhere near the hull. Eddie finds this out at the same moment they do: *"I've got a number! I've — oh. You can't ring it from there, can you. Well. Come inside and I'll ring someone for you."*

What has actually changed:

- **Eddie is on the Aura network,** as a **user**. Aura is a network, not a mind ([The Aura Network](../../World/The-Aura-Network.md)) — towers, permissions, and a voice you ask things of. Jak holds the top keys, so Eddie's first real conversation with the outside world is, within the hour, with the Administrator.
- **The void has a voice.** Teleportation and scrying are still blocked and the network still can't *see inside* the ship. But someone inside can **talk out**, and Jak can talk in. This is what Jak sent the party south for ([Jak Bjornsson](../../NPCs/Jak-Bjornsson.md)).
- **The ship can make and take calls — through Eddie.** The Aura link terminates in the ship's own systems, so a call is placed *by Eddie* and heard on the intercom: [Erleena](../../NPCs/Erleena-Riser.md) stops being unreachable, George can ring Frostwatch, and anyone outside with a working phone can ring the ship. **It is not a phone tower.** Handsets still don't work aboard or near the hull; nobody walks the decks with a phone to their ear. (Every call runs through Aura, which means through Jak's permissions.)
- **This is how the book gets out.** Erleena's *[A Brief History of Vermoon](../../Aerun-Players-Guide/A-Brief-History-of-Vermoon.md)* — typeset and date-checked by "E." — goes out to the world over this link.
- **A tell, for a table that's listening:** Eddie needs no translator. *"It's like hearing your grandchildren's slang. I don't know the words, but I know the grammar."* ([The Ship's Purpose](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Ships-Purpose.md)). Don't explain it; Erleena will, if asked.

## The serious bit (for the DM, not the scene)

Don't let any of this slow the comedy down — but plugging the ship into Aura touches three live threads, and the party may see that before they pick up the dish. Let them argue at the airlock, not on the mountain. George and Erleena are **for** it: outside contact was half the reason they woke Eddie.

- **The withheld theory.** The party has been keeping the network-corruption theory from "anyone with a glowing ear" ([The Invisible Infection](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Invisible-Infection.md)). If something rides the network, the ship's mind is now on it too. Mark's call; doesn't need making today.
- **Jak's sealed orders** ([Tomas Vel-Maret](../../NPCs/Tomas-Vel-Maret.md) has the copy). A ship that can simply be *asked* changes what those orders are for.
- **Eddie doesn't know what he's joining.** He wants to get a message out because he was asked to. Play his delight straight.

If the party says no, Eddie is disappointed, says so once, and drops it. The box stays on the shelf.

## Rewards

- **Eddie's favor,** paid — the medical decks open if they haven't already.
- **Outside comms from the ship,** for the rest of the arc and for George's holding action afterward: the party can call the ship from anywhere the network reaches — Aerun's coastal cities included, though nothing beyond the Rim Wall.
- The box. Eddie suggests they keep it. *"For next time."*

## Running it

Thirty minutes, and it should be **ridiculous**: a centaur with a satellite dish on his back, a breadbox on a four-hundred-foot lead giving confident wrong instructions, a sledgehammer, three blue wires, and a bird sitting on the thing you're trying to aim. Nobody is in real danger and everybody is having a terrible time. Roll *And Whatnot* every round without mercy, keep Eddie talking over everything, ask **"cut or wait?"** faster than they can think — and cut to quiet the instant the lamps go green.
