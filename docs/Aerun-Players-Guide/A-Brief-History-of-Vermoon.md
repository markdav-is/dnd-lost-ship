# A Brief History of Vermoon

*By Professor Erleena Riser, Academy of the Fettered Mind (on extended leave). A short book for people who have walked past the old ruins their whole lives and never been told what they are.*

**Player-facing and spoiler-free**, like everything in this section. This page is the book's highlights: the chapters in brief, the timeline from the inside cover, and a few of the lines people quote. Your character can have read it. Most of Vermoon is about to.

> *"I am an archaeologist, which means I spend my life being wrong in an organized way. Everything in this book is the best account I can give today. Where I am guessing, I say so. Where nobody knows, I say that too — and those are my favorite pages."*
> — from the preface

## About the book

- **It is short on purpose.** Nine small chapters and a timeline. The Professor says she wrote it for the baker in Yorrem who asked her what the big metal spire in Oldspire *was*, and looked embarrassed for asking.
- **It is new.** She had been working on it for years between digs. It came out this season — copied and set, the acknowledgments say, *"with the patient help of E., who corrected my dates without being asked, and was right every time."*
- **It is about everybody.** Titans, dragons, the ancients, the fall, the guilds, and the two strange years everyone just lived through — told in order, as one story, which nobody had done before.

## The timeline

*From the inside cover. The Professor's note underneath reads: "The top half of this page is legend and the bottom half is news. The interesting part is the middle, where one turns into the other."*

| When | What happened |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Before counting** | The four **Titans** shape the world: **Kaia** of earth, **Savita** of fire, **Ermir** of air, **Dwyn** of water. They make the **dragons** first, to keep the peace, and then everything else |
| **The long quiet** | The First Age. The Titans walk the world and look after it. The dragons keep order. By every account it was an easy job |
| **The ship** | A starship limps out of the sky, barely holding together. Its people — the **Antari**, humans fleeing a war on their own world — ask to stay. The Titans and dragons say yes |
| **The good years** | The Antari share science and medicine and get along with their neighbors. They also bring new gods, new wants, and the idea of *not having enough* |
| **The quarrel** | The Antari spread past the land they were given. The Titans and dragons keep stepping in. Finally the Antari are asked to leave |
| **The taking** | Instead of leaving, the Antari capture the Titans — **Savita first** — and draw power from them like water from a well. The dragons, whose strength is tied to the Titans, are laid low |
| **The cities of metal** | On that stolen power the Antari build **Dirtroit** (earth), **Eldorado** (fire), **Airlantis** (air), and **Oceana** (water), a station in the sky, and **Aura** — the great network that tied them all together |
| **The Fall — about 720 years ago** | The **Red Dragon** ends it. She gathers the power of the other great dragons into colored **orbs**, sets them around the world, and breaks them. The cities are buried, drowned, or lost. The lights go out |
| **The dark years** | Nobody wrote anything down. People survived. That is all we know, and it is enough to be grateful for |
| **The Second Age** | The guilds rise — the **Gleaners**, the **Tinkerers**, the **Academy**, the **Devout Coalition** — and govern together through **the Board**. The ruins become scenery. The cities become bedtime stories |
| **Two years ago** | The **Prophecy of the Red Dragon** wakes. A band of strangers — **Wayward, the Friends** (yes, "WTF"; they chose it) — share one dream and then one road. The Aura network comes back on. The power comes back. The lost cities are found, one by one |
| **Two years ago, the end of it** | Something old in the sky station tries to take the world back, and fails. The Blue Dragon leaves for the stars. The other dragons go back to sleep |
| **Since** | **Jak Bjornsson**, one of the Friends, takes control of the Aura network and begins **Jak's Peace** — the one we enjoy today. He rules in the open, from Grønnfjord. The network is the Antari's. So are the phones |

## The chapters

### 1. What are all these ruins?

They are one people's. Every metal spire, every smooth-walled room under a hill, every door with no handle: **Antari**, all of it, and all about the same age. Most folk call them "the ancients" or "the Fallen." The Professor prefers their own name, which she read off their own walls.

> *"We have been living in somebody else's house for seven hundred years and arguing about who gets the good chair."*

### 2. The Titans and the dragons

The oldest story we have, told plainly: four Titans made the world and loved it, and made the dragons first so there would be someone to look after it. Four great dragons each guarded one Titan — **Essos** the black for Kaia, **Claire** the silver for Ermir, **Dildro** the blue for Dwyn, and the Red Dragon for Savita. A fifth, **Undaloth** the green, guarded no Titan and answered to no one, which the Professor says tells you most of what you need to know about him.

She is careful to call this chapter *what the dragons say*. She has met several of them. She reports that they are truthful, proud, and not interested in being interviewed.

### 3. The people who fell out of the sky

The Antari were not monsters. The book is firm about this. They were frightened people on a broken ship who were shown kindness, and for a good while they returned it.

> *"They brought us medicine. They also brought us the notion that your neighbor having something means you have less. We kept both."*

### 4. How you steal a god

The short, grim chapter. The Antari learned that a Titan could *power* things. They took Savita first and held her so the others would not fight back, then took the rest. They never killed them. A dead Titan lights nothing.

### 5. The cities

One for each Titan, plus the one in the sky. The Professor has stood in every one of them — the old gates put you in a single room of each — and, between her own digs and what the Friends told her, describes three like a tourist who cannot believe her luck: **Oceana**, a glass city a mile under the sea that could turn the ocean's currents like a tap; **Airlantis**, a flying city the size of a festival ground; **Dirtroit**, dropped whole under a mountain range with its streetlights still on. **Eldorado**, the forge city, gets a single short paragraph: *"I have seen one room of it. It was extremely hot and all the doors were locked. I expect more of the same down there, only warmer. I am saving it."*

### 6. The Fall

The Red Dragon could not free the Titans, so she broke the world that held them. Every great dragon gave up their power to do it. Undaloth died setting his orb in place. The book does not pretend this was clean.

> *"People ask me whether the Red Dragon was right. I dig things up; I don't hand down verdicts. But I'll tell you what I tell my students: there is no version of that story where everyone gets to keep what they had."*

### 7. The Second Age

Our age. The guilds, the Board, the Academy's chains, the Gleaners' harvest festival at Yorrem. The Professor's view is that the Second Age's best idea was *nobody gets to be in charge of everything*, and she notes — politely, and only once — that this idea is currently being tested.

### 8. The two years everybody remembers

The part you may have lived through, from someone who was in the room for a good deal of it: the dream; Wayward, the Friends; the dig called **the Void**, where the Aura network came back on after 721 years and its voice asked, very courteously, for help with the power supply; the dead towers coming back; the tides and storms going wrong as something in the sky pulled the old cities' levers; Oceana, Airlantis, the station; and the end of it — **Jak Bjornsson taking control of the network, and the start of Jak's Peace.**

She takes a page to explain what Aura *is*, because people get it wrong. **Aura is less a mind than a network** — wires and towers and permissions, the thing the phones talk through. The pleasant voice is how you ask it for things. It does what whoever holds the keys tells it to, and no more.

> *"People ask me what Aura wants. Aura doesn't want. Ask instead who is holding the keys — and these days, happily, we know his name."*

There is a page on **Undaloth**, whom the Antari had rebuilt as a machine and whom the Professor helped put back in his own body. It is the only place in the book where she sounds sentimental, and she apologizes for it in a footnote.

On the **Blue Dragon** she is brief and fond. He had been walking among us in a man's shape for years, nudging things along. He decided Vermoon was not where he was meant to be, and he went to find out where was.

### 9. What we still don't know

The best chapter, by her own account. A few of its questions:

- **Did the Titans really make everything?** The Antari argued they couldn't have — that was how they talked half of Vermoon into helping them. The argument was self-serving. *That does not make it wrong.* The Titans are asleep and the dragons change the subject.
- **Where is the Red Dragon?**
- **What is under Eldorado?**
- **Why is Aerun a desert?** *"Aerun's people have their own answer and I have no business correcting them from an armchair. I am going there to listen. If there is a second edition, that is where it will come from."*

## Lines people quote

- *"History is just gossip that held still long enough to be checked."*
- *"If a door has no handle, it was not built for you. Knock anyway."*
- *"The Antari did not fall because they were clever. Plenty of us are clever. They fell because they could not stand to be told no."*
- On the cover, from a colleague identified only as G.: *"Readable. I am as surprised as anyone."*

## What the book adds, by lore check

Your character may simply have read the book — everything above is free. Rolling (Intelligence, History) gets at what a careful reader, or someone who knows the Professor's field, takes from it.

| DC | What you know |
| :-: | :--- |
| **10** | The telephones, the network, and the portals are all Antari work that came back on when the power did. The fallen spire that **Oldspire** is built inside was one of their speaking-towers |
| **15** | **Lost Rock**, the wandering spire in Taldea's poisoned swamp, is no rock: it is the tip of the horn of **Kaia**, the Earth Titan, who lies asleep under the Bolt Mountains. The Antari used it as a tower. The book says so in a footnote most readers miss |
| **20** | The portal rings are marked with five symbols, one for each city and one for the sky station, and an Antari emblem is the key. The Professor can read a good deal of the Antari script and says the rest is a matter of time |
| **25** | **Rumor — the DM decides.** *There was a fifth Titan, and the Professor left it out on purpose.* · *Some of the Antari never died; they are asleep in the sky, waiting.* · *The Red Dragon is alive and has read the book.* · *Eldorado's streets really are gold.* · *The Blue Dragon is coming back, and not alone.* |
