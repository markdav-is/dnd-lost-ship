# Aerun: The Desert Continent

*A comprehensive guide to the central continent of Vermoon, its political structure, the deep desert, and the secret ecology of the Spice.*

## Geography: The Concentric Rings

Aerun is the central equatorial continent of Vermoon, characterized by a concentric geographical structure. The environment grows progressively more hostile the further inland one travels, creating three distinct zones of habitation and culture.

### 1. The Coastal Ring and The City-States

The outer edge of Aerun is a habitable coastal ring dominated by seven great city-states. These cities control the global commerce of Vermoon, serving as the essential shipping hubs between the northern continent (Lafdea), the Scarlands, and the other landmasses.

The cities are connected by the **Ring Road** — a massive literal highway that circles the entire continent, facilitating the movement of bulk goods that cannot be transported via the Aura network's teleportation systems. While Jak (the Administrator) maintains small temples in these cities, the true power lies with the noble houses that control the trade routes. Each city-state is dominated by one of the seven great merchant houses:

| City-State | Ruling House | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Balic** | House Wavir | Merchant-lord democracy; silt harbor fleet; salt and silver trade |
| **Draj** | House Tsalaxa | Theocratic warrior culture; god-king figurehead; hemp and grain exports |
| **Gulg** | House Inika | Forest city; sorcerer-queen styles herself a goddess; druidic templars |
| **Nibenay** | House Shom | Shadow King rules in mystery; half-giant legions; rare wood exports |
| **Raam** | House M'ke | Teetering on civil war after sorcerer-queen's death; armed factions vie for control |
| **Tyr** | House Vordon | The Free City; first to abolish slavery; council-governed; iron mines |
| **Urik** | House Stel | Militarized warrior-state; sorcerer-king still in power; sealed borders |

### 2. The Rim Wall West

Separating the coastal ring from the interior is a massive geological barrier known as the Rim Wall. It is the final boundary of civilization. Beyond the Rim Wall, the Aura network fails completely, rendering Jak blind to everything that happens in the interior. The coastal citizens consider everything beyond the wall to be uninhabitable wasteland.

### 3. The Deep Desert and the Tablelands

The center of Aerun is the Deep Desert — a realm of capricious, violent sandstorms and absolute water scarcity. It is entirely off the grid. To the coastal cities, it is a dead zone. To the deep desert tribes (the Fremen-analogues like Kaelen al-Hajra), it is home, a place of harsh religious discipline and survival.

Between the Rim Wall and the true Deep Desert lie the **Tablelands** — a transitional zone of varied, hostile terrain:

- **Stony Barrens:** Vast stretches of exposed bedrock and loose stones, scoured by wind. Almost nothing grows here.
- **Sandy Wastes:** Rolling dunes of yellow and orange sand that shift constantly, burying ancient ruins.
- **Rocky Badlands:** Mazes of eroded stone pillars and deep canyons. Raiders use these as lairs, striking at caravans from hidden vantage points.
- **Scrub Plains:** Rare areas that support tough, thorny vegetation, often fiercely protected by desert druids.
- **The Silt Sea:** In certain deep basins, the sand gives way to a sea of fine, powdery dust that flows like water. Navigable only by psionic silt skimmers. Horrific creatures lurk beneath the surface.

## The Secret Ecology: Spores, Worms, and Spice

The defining truth of Aerun — known only to the deep desert tribes and hidden from both the coastal nobles and Jak himself — is that the Deep Desert is not a natural phenomenon. It is a **quarantine zone**.

### The First Source

Buried somewhere in the Deep Desert is the "First Source" — an ancient, catastrophic origin point for a fungal/spore infection identical to the one currently emerging from the crashed ship in the Barrier Peaks. The desertification of Aerun was the mechanism of containment. The desert did not happen to Aerun; it was *made*, at terrible cost, to keep something buried.

### The Lifecycle

The ecology of the Deep Desert is a closed loop that keeps the First Source contained:

1. **Spawn:** Spores spawn from the First Source.
2. **Mass:** Without anything organic to attach to in the barren desert, the spores gather into dense masses.
3. **Bloom:** The mass eventually explodes into a violent fungal bloom.
4. **Consumption:** The dead spores and fungal matter are consumed by the apex predators of the deep desert: the great worms.

### The Worms

The coastal cities are aware of smaller, juvenile worms that occasionally breach the Rim Wall, viewing them as dangerous but manageable desert fauna. The massive, ancient worms of the Deep Desert — the true consumers of the spore blooms — are considered myth by the city-dwellers. To the deep desert tribes, they are revered forces of nature that maintain the balance of the world. Without the worms, the cycle breaks and the desert itself would eventually be consumed.

### The Spice and The Way

The byproduct of the worms consuming the dead spores is the **Spice**.

- **The Economy:** Spice is the most valuable resource on Vermoon, generating immense wealth for the coastal noble houses who export it globally.
- **The Effect:** Consumption of the Spice enables and amplifies psionic abilities — known as "the Will and the Way." Almost all beings on Aerun possess minor psionic wild talents from ambient Spice exposure; concentrated use unlocks true mastery.
- **The Mark:** Heavy, sustained use stains the sclera and iris a deep, uniform blue — an unmistakable sign of a serious practitioner.

The coastal nobles believe Spice is a naturally occurring mineral or botanical resource. The fact that it is the processed byproduct of an apocalyptic fungal infection and giant desert worms is the most closely guarded secret of the deep desert tribes.

## Magic: Preservers and Defilers

Arcane magic on Aerun is intrinsically tied to the life force of the planet. Wizards fall into two distinct camps:

**Preservers** draw only enough energy from surrounding plants to cast their spells, leaving the flora to recover. Because all magic is viewed with suspicion, Preservers often operate in secret cells known as the **Veiled Alliance** — a network that works quietly toward restoring Aerun's lost greenery.

**Defilers** rip the life force from the surrounding area, turning fertile soil to sterile ash and killing plant life in a visible radius. The sorcerer-kings are defilers of the highest order, combining destructive magic with powerful psionics. Their centuries of defiling are a primary reason the Tablelands are as barren as they are. Arcane magic is widely feared and often illegal in the city-states, punishable by death or enslavement.

## Society and Factions

### The Social Hierarchy

| Tier | Group | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sorcerer-Kings** | God-like immortal rulers; masters of defiling magic and psionics |
| 2 | **Templars** | Bureaucratic enforcers; manage water distribution; granted minor magic by their king |
| 3 | **Nobility / Merchant Houses** | Control farms, water, Spice, and the Ring Road |
| 4 | **Free Citizens** | Artisans, crafters, minor merchants; freedom is tenuous |
| 5 | **Slaves** | The economic foundation; field workers, miners, gladiators |

In cities where the sorcerer-king has fallen — Tyr, Raam, Balic, Draj — this hierarchy is in violent flux. **Gladiatorial arenas** in nearly every city-state serve as entertainment and social control; a successful gladiator is one of the only paths of upward mobility available to the enslaved.

### The Races of Aerun

- **Humans:** Dominant in the city-states; adaptable and ruthlessly ambitious.
- **Elves:** Nomadic desert runners; viewed with suspicion in cities as untrustworthy merchants and thieves — a reputation largely earned.
- **Dwarves:** Hairless and intensely focused on a single life-goal ("focus"); unmatched builders and laborers.
- **Muls:** A sterile human-dwarf crossbreed, bred for the arenas and heavy labor; human ambition combined with dwarven stamina.
- **Thri-Kreen:** Giant mantis-like insectoids who roam the deep wastes in hunting packs; perfectly adapted to the desert; rarely seen in cities except as slaves or adventuring companions.
- **Half-Giants:** Massive shock troops employed by the sorcerer-kings; emotionally volatile and easily influenced.

## Political Dynamics: The Balance of Power

**Jak and the Aura Network** — Jak controls teleportation and instant global communication. He sees and hears everything on the grid. Beyond the Rim Wall, he is blind.

**The Noble Alliance** — The seven merchant houses control the Ring Road, the Spice trade, and the bulk shipping that keeps every city alive. They are quietly forming a Landsraad-style alliance against Jak's growing authoritarian control. The recent fall of sorcerer-kings in Tyr, Raam, Balic, and Draj has dramatically shifted this balance. The surviving sorcerer-kings of Urik, Gulg, and Nibenay are consolidating power while the leaderless cities have become arenas of factional conflict.

## The Barrier Peaks Connection

The crashed ship in the Scarlands is a **second spore source**. Because it crashed in the cold, wet, organic-rich Scarlands mountains:

1. The spores have abundant life to attach to immediately — they do not need to mass before blooming.
2. There are no desert worms in the Scarlands to consume the blooms.

Without the worms to close the lifecycle, the infection will spread unchecked. Kaelen al-Hajra recognizes it immediately. The only thing keeping Aerun from being consumed is the brutal ecology of the worms and the desert — an ecology the Scarlands does not have. The party's actions in the Barrier Peaks are not merely a local adventure. They are, unknowingly, fighting the same war Aerun has been fighting for millennia.
