# Chapter 5b: The Refusal

*You wake with the memory and not the gift.*

Dawn in the ring of stones. The gray to every horizon. Your guide, awake before you, watching the place where the sun comes up.

You remember all of it. The vision, the beast, the thing it held out to you. You remember turning from it, and you remember that it let you. It did not argue. Nothing in the desert argues.

Your eyes are your own color. The guide looks, and sees that, and says nothing about it. The walk down is the same walk.

## What stays

The beast's shape stays. Not every night. But some nights, at the edge of sleep, there it is: the eight legs, or the hood of silk, or the shadow of a thing too big to be in a dream. It is not waiting. It is simply where you left it.

## Once

The desert gives once. Every native will tell you so. What they do not always say is that *once* is not *now*.

You may come back. A year, the old women say — a year at least, and the same road, and the same asking, and a guide who may or may not be the same one. The beast will be. It has not moved. It does not change its mind, because it never made one up; it only offered, and it will offer again.

Whether you take the offer the second time is the only part of this that was ever yours.
