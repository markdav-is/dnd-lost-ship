## scene: interview-1 [dry]

INTERN: Professor Erleena Riser, of the Academy of the Fettered Mind. She's speaking from the terminal set at Balic, down at the end of the Long Reach, which is not a thing the Guild lets outlanders do. Professor, how are you on my radio?

ERLEENA: [wry] The Fettered Mind has a standing arrangement with the Guild for its Balic house. I asked nicely and signed something in triplicate.

INTERN: [laughs] You'll do fine here. Start me at the beginning. Why write a history for people who aren't historians?

ERLEENA: Because of a baker in Yorrem. She stopped me in the street and asked what the big metal spire in Oldspire actually was. She'd walked past it her entire life. And she looked embarrassed for asking. [beat] That did it. No one should have to feel small for not knowing a thing they were never told.

INTERN: So what is it?

ERLEENA: A tower for talking across the world. Built by a people called the Antari, along with nearly every old ruin you have ever stepped over, and it went dark with the rest of their world about seven hundred and twenty years ago. Most folk say "the ancients." I use the name they used, because I read it off their own walls.

INTERN: We get their leavings here. Glass that bends. Metal that remembers its shape. The salvage yards sell it by the basket.

ERLEENA: [dry] Then you already own more of my subject than my academy does. There's a line in the book about that: we have been living in somebody else's house for seven centuries, and arguing about who gets the good chair.

## scene: interview-2 [dry]

INTERN: Give me the whole history. Short. Pretend I'm the baker.

ERLEENA: [a breath, enjoying it] In the beginning there were four Titans. Earth, fire, air, water. They shaped the world, and the first thing they made was dragons, to keep the peace. For a very long time that was an easy job. [beat] Then a ship fell out of the sky. Broken, barely holding together, full of frightened people running from a war on their own world. They asked to stay. The Titans said yes.

INTERN: That's the kind part.

ERLEENA: It is, and I want your listeners to hear it, because the book is firm on this: the Antari were not monsters. They brought medicine. They also brought the idea that your neighbor having something means you have less. We kept both.

INTERN: And then?

ERLEENA: Then they were asked to leave, and found out instead that a Titan could power a city. So they took the Titans. One at a time. Never killing them. A dead Titan lights nothing. [pause] On that stolen power they built four great cities, one in the sky, one under the sea, and a network to tie it all together. It held until the Red Dragon decided otherwise, and broke the world it was standing on. We call that the Fall.

INTERN: Was she right to?

ERLEENA: [gently] People ask me that at every lecture. I dig things up. I don't hand down verdicts. But I tell my students this much: there is no telling of that story where everybody gets to keep what they had.

INTERN: The network. Aura. Out here people talk about it like it's a person. What does it want?

ERLEENA: [firmly, amused] It doesn't. That's the most useful page in the book and it's barely a page. Aura is wires and towers and permissions, and a pleasant voice you ask things of. It does what whoever holds the keys tells it. So don't ask what Aura wants. Ask who's holding the keys. [beat] These days, happily, we know his name, and the peace is his.

## scene: interview-3 [dry]

INTERN: Your last chapter is called "What We Still Don't Know." That's a brave way to end a history.

ERLEENA: It's the only honest one. I'm an archaeologist. I spend my life being wrong in an organized way. [beat] Did the Titans truly make everything? The Antari argued they couldn't have, and the argument was self-serving, and that does not make it false. Where is the Red Dragon now? I don't know. The other dragons change the subject.

INTERN: And the one every listener on this lane is waiting for. Why is Aerun a desert?

ERLEENA: [laughs, tired] No. I stepped off a ship this week. Your people have their own account of that, and I have no business correcting it from an armchair in somebody's guest room. Ask me in a year. I came to listen.

INTERN: [quietly pleased] I said something on this radio a while back. That this continent is the hub every chart runs to, and almost nobody born here ever leaves along a spoke.

ERLEENA: I came in along one. It's a perfectly good road, Westerly. It runs both ways.

INTERN: [beat] ...Huh. [recovering] One more. Your chapter on the lost cities gives the forge city a single paragraph. Eldorado. Professor, we have an Eldorado. It's a town up behind Tyr. It sells rope.

ERLEENA: [delighted] So I'm told. I intend to buy some.

INTERN: [laughs] Where does a person get the book?

ERLEENA: The Fettered Mind, in Balic. Guilders' Precinct, the building with the columns. I brought a box with me. [dry] I did not carry the box. I'd like to thank the people who did, none of whom are speaking to me.

INTERN: Professor Erleena Riser. A Brief History of Vermoon. Last word's yours.

ERLEENA: [warmly] It's from the book. If a door has no handle, it wasn't built for you. Knock anyway.
