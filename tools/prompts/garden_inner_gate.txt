Eye-level view along a narrow flagstone path through the outer garden, the jungle pressing close on both sides with huge alien fronds and tall trees, bright sunlight in shafts through the canopy. The path runs downhill over two shallow terrace steps to a 20-foot wall of living grass and vines that cuts across the view, broken by a round gate chamber — a squat cylindrical structure of pale ship panels half-swallowed by growth, its round doorway standing open with a small green lamp beside it. Through the open doorway: a glimpse of the inner garden beyond, darker and hazier, a field of pale bristly corkscrew grass along the path and, further, the flat black shine of a lake. The russet spore haze is ankle-deep here and noticeably thicker through the doorway. The ceiling is exactly the same as in the reference images: one continuous shallow dome of radial pale metal ribs with bright light between them, and the single round cylindrical sun-lamp fixture at its centre. It is 80 feet up and far away, seen only in a slice above the treetops at the top of the frame, much smaller and more distant than the gate. No octagonal skylight, no low ceiling, no glass.
