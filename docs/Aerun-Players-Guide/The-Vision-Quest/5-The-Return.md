# Chapter 5: The Return

*The walk down is the same walk. You are not.*

You go back the way you came, or another way; the guide decides, reading the wind. The wall is just a wall on the way down. Your legs know it. Your eyes keep going to things a moment early: a stone about to turn under the guide's heel, a kestrekel about to lift off the rim.

You do not talk much. Neither does the guide. On the second day, near the foot of the wall, the guide says the only thing the guide is going to say about it.

**If your guide was the old woman**, she says: "You told me a true thing. Now you have one more you can tell no one. That is all the desert ever gives — one more thing that is only yours." She goes back up the slope to her hut and does not watch you leave.

**If your guide was the hooded runaway**, he says nothing at all until the edge of the salt flat, and then, without turning: "The tube. A waystation. Any waystation. Do not be long about it." And then he is walking back toward the theater, and you understand he will not be in Salt View when you return.

**If your guide was the kreen hunter**, the hunter stops a long way short of the well and looks at you with the whole face. "First use. Not for you. I will know." Then the hunter is gone into the scrub the way the hunter came out of it, and you could not say when.

## The village

The well-keeper, the pit-boss, the factor — whoever pointed you — looks at you as you come in and looks a moment longer than they need to. They have seen it before.

A thin ring of blue at the edge of the iris. Not the deep stain of the Guild's Navigators or the desert people. Just a rim. Most people will never notice. The ones who do will know exactly what it means.

Your guide's price comes due now. The tube. The first use. The thing you said out loud. The desert keeps no ledgers, but the guides do.

## What comes after

The Schools notice. They always notice. You may be on the Ring Road for a month, or a season, and then there is a woman in gray at the next waystation who does not quite look at you, or a man with ink on his fingers who already knows your name and what you paid at the well. A Stillwater, or a Reckoner, come asking. What they ask is up to them. What you say is up to you.
