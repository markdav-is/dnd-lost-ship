# Ecology of the Silt Sea

The continent's heart is a dead ocean that is *not dead at all* — it is a **managed organism**, a thousand-year-old machine of dust, spores, worms, and prayer whose product is the Spice and whose purpose is the [First Source's](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source.md) imprisonment. This page is the DM's systems reference: how the plateau works, what lives on it, and where civilization dares to touch it.

## The plateau — the dead sea on the roof of the continent

The silt lies **on top of a colossal central plateau** — a flat-topped tableland whose upper surface *is* the Sea of Silt: the bed of the Green Age's rain-fed **heart-sea**, unmade in the defiling. Think of a Monument Valley mesa scaled to half a continent, its tabletop a smooth gray dust plain. Its outline is **lobed, not oval** — most dramatically **the Prong**, a vast blunt promontory thrown southeast between Raam and Balic that shoulders the eastern coast aside and gives the Crescent Forest its curve. The real-world analog is the **Salar de Uyuni**: a vast dead sea-bed high in the mountains, flat, pale, and dry *because* it is high. Nothing flows up to it; everything it ever held has been draining out through the rock for a thousand years.

*(Not in this table on purpose: the **Dragon's Bowl** lies **outside the Rim Wall**, in the living-side Tablelands north of Urik between the caravan routes — a separate thousand-foot basin with Lake Pit's true water at its heart. The silt never touched it; that is rather the point of it.)*

**How the wall should read** (the southwestern atlas crop nailed it): tall columnar cliff-organs of ochre rock, and the flat gray sitting *on top of* them — the silt's edge is the cliff's crest, never a shoreline at ground level. **Silt-falls:** wind pushes the silt over the rim's low points in slow, permanent gray cascades — the arms are frozen spill-lobes stepping down through the gaps, and the smallest falls smoke off the crest wherever the wind runs. From the coastal ring you look **up** at the wall, and on a hard wind-day you can see the gray breathing over the top.

**Orientation (per the Aerun Atlas):** unlike Athas — where the silt lies east of the cities — Aerun's silt mass sits **center-north** of the continent, and the widest living band with the great ocean front runs along the **south**. The continental rule of thumb: **silt to the north, ocean to the south.** Per city: **Balic** (south coast) faces ocean south, silt north up the great ramp — the rule exactly. **Raam** (northeast coast) inverts locally: ocean on its northeast face, the silt arm at its southwest back. **Draj** (north coast) is pinched between the two: ocean a few miles north behind it, its mud-shelf arm spreading south toward the deeps. **Tyr and Urik** keep the silt at their eastern backs beyond the mountains; **Nibenay and Gulg** at their western backs beyond the Badlands.

The silt itself is powder finer than flour — it **flows like water and drowns like water**. A person sinks; a mekillot sinks slower; nothing swims. Crossing means Guild **silt-crawlers**, Balic-built **silt skimmers and schooners** (psionically buoyed), giant-wading channels, or the [Sandwalkers'](../Factions/The-Sandwalkers.md) secret **shoals** — ridges of firmer sediment that shift with the seasons and are read like tides.

## Why it stays dry (the weather engine)

1. **Rain shadow.** Like the Atacama beside the Pacific: ocean winds rain out on the coastal ranges and the Rim Wall, and descend inside hot and empty. The coastal ring is green *because* the interior is dry — the mountains harvest the whole continent's rain for the coast.
2. **The haze dome.** The silt makes its own weather: the vast hot plain lofts a permanent particulate haze that suppresses rainfall outright — droplets cannot form in that much dust. Storms that crest the wall rain a brief crust of mud at the plateau's edge and die. The **silt-storms** are this engine venting: dust hurricanes that rearrange the shoals and bury the unwary.
3. **The Drought (the true layer).** Water is the spore vector — the Green Age walls fell to spores in the *groundwater*. Elevation makes the quarantine self-keeping: **nothing flows up**, rain that falls drains *out* through the rock, and the worms' larval stage **sequesters** what lingers in the deeps. The dryness is the quarantine's outermost wall — and the drainage is the continent's darkest open secret: **the coastal ring's springs are the dead sea bleeding out**. Nibenay's bubbling hot springs, Tyr's deep aquifer, the seeps that make Draj's mudflats — the coast drinks the murdered sea, filtered through a thousand years of stone. (Filtered *enough* — the wardens test the springs in ways the cities never see.) **Wetting the interior at scale is a containment breach** — the wardens' driest law.

## The food chain (the containment cycle)

There is exactly one economy in the deep silt, and it runs on the enemy:

1. **Spawn** — spores rise from the First Source, far below.
2. **Mass** — with nothing living to infect, they clump into drifting masses.
3. **Bloom** — the masses detonate into violent fungal blooms across the silt surface.
4. **Consumption** — **the great worms** rise and feed on the dead blooms; nothing else on Vermoon can digest them.
5. **The Spice** — the worms' castings, harvested from the shoals: psionics, long life, blue eyes, and the fortunes of the [seven families](../Factions/The-Merchant-Houses.md).

Everything else in the silt lives on this chain's margins: **silt horrors** ambush along the arms and shallows (tentacled things that erupt under skimmers); **silt runners and floaters** scavenge bloom-edges; **cloud rays and silt drakes** ride the haze dome hunting whatever surfaces; **silt giants** wade the ramp-shallows spearing what the horrors miss. Rule of thumb for encounters: **life clusters where the silt is shallow or the blooms are fresh** — the open deeps are empty, silent, and veiled.

**Rain-years are bloom-years:** the rare storm that breaches the dome supercharges the cycle. The coast experiences this only as a bumper Spice harvest and never asks why. The wardens experience it as a siege.

## The wardens' layer

The [Sandwalkers](../Factions/The-Sandwalkers.md) walk the shoals, read the currents, cull blooms that drift toward the arms, and maintain [the Veil](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source.md) that hides the deeps from every approaching mind. Their law, in order: nothing wet, nothing charted, nothing woken. They tolerate the Guild's crossing lanes and the harvest shoals; they forbid one place only — and no one who crosses it remembers there was anything to forbid.

## Where civilization touches the silt

**How a portage works (the mechanics — no magic required):** the climbs are conquered the Dark Sun way — **with backs**. At the transfer yards, argosy containers and ships' holds are **broken into man-loads and beast-loads**: endless files of porters on the ramp-stairs with chitin back-frames, mekillot wagon-teams on the switchback grades, cargo re-consolidated at the top. Only the *heaviest single pieces* — hull timbers, obsidian blocks, the Guild's sealed Spice vaults — ride the **hoists**: short counterweight lifts (**~250–300 ft per tier**, the proven limit of pre-steel engineering — Meteora's monks ran higher on rope alone) strung with **giant-hair rope** over chitin sheaves, trimmed by mekillot capstans. The counterweight is the setting's best trick: **the silt itself** — a descending skip is filled from a sluice at the top, its weight raises the freight, and the dumped ballast blows home on the wind to the sea that did the work. A hired psion riding as telekinetic brake is the Guild's premium service, not the mechanism. Every hoist-yard lives under its own gray dribble of spilled ballast; the porters' cough has a name in both cities.

**The two portage cities** — the only places the living economy and the dead sea meet at scale, both perched on the rim with a harbor on each face:

- **[Raam](../../Reference/Aerun-City-Map-Roster.md) — the northeastern portage, a city climbing its own wall.** **Ocean harbor on the northeast face** at sea level (the deepwater docks and Ring Road exchange); the **Silt Transfer on the southwest shelf ~1,000 ft above**. Between them, Raam is **built into the wall itself**: the sprawl at the foot gives way to the **Ascent Quarter** — switchback freight terraces cut into the cliff, with warehouses, porter barracks, toll-houses, and whole tenement galleries **carved into the rock faces** between the bends. Mekillot teams and porter files carry the trade up ~700 ft of stepped road; the final **~300-ft cliff band** is worked by **silt-ballast hoists** beside zigzag porter-stairs hewn into the stone. The hoist-heads are Guild property; the porter guilds of the Ascent are a political force; and in a city this lawless, the climb is the one thing *nobody* riots near.
- **[Balic](../../Reference/Aerun-City-Map-Roster.md) — the southern portage, the city inside the dam.** Balic reads as **Minas Tirith built into the Rim Wall** — concentric walled tiers climbing the rock, a great dividing buttress, switchback ramps — except the summit is a dust-harbor instead of a citadel. It is **built into the dam itself** — the rock barrier at the foot of the southern silt-ramp, the one place the silt found a path downhill toward the sea and stopped. **Ocean harbor on the south face** at sea level (white quays, breakwater lighthouses); **silt quays on the dam's crest ~700 ft above**, skimmers moored on the gray over the city's rooftops; and between them the dam's face is **terraced with the city** — precincts, villas, and theaters stepping up the rock. The portage runs *through* the dam: **the Cochlea** — a colossal **spiral porter-stair bored down through the living rock** (think of a screw turned through the dam), wide enough for two laden crodlu abreast, turning past **market landings, toll gates, and lamplit galleries at every revolution** — an endless double helix of porters, up-file and down-file, that never stops turning, day or night. The exterior **Stair of Sails** crane galleries handle the heavy pieces by silt-ballast hoist. Half of Balic earns its bread on its back, and the city measures status by *which turn of the Cochlea you live off*. Nothing sails through; everything portages, and Balic taxes the seam — the richest toll on Vermoon. **The sill is the most dangerous rock on the continent:** breach it and the hanging silt comes down — a gray avalanche that flows like water, spore-laden, racing through the city toward the sea. Not a flood; a containment failure with an address. The wardens know. The Sere know. Balic's engineers pray nobody else works it out.

**The Raam–Balic lanes:** the Guild's marked **shipping lanes** cross the open silt between the two portages — **~350 miles of the continent's diagonal, 5–6 days by convoy** against 3–4 weeks around the half-ring by road. Bone-and-obsidian channel markers stake the route along the **eastern and southern reaches**; convoys of skimmers and schooners run it under Guild schedule and warden sufferance, with harvest shoals branching off like sidings. Lane law is simple: stay between the markers, pay the rate, stop for nothing that hails you from the gray. **The lanes never bend northwest** — see below.

**The Deeps:** the **northwestern reach** of the sea is the unplace — no lanes, no markers, no charts, and no memory: [the Veil](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source.md) holds there, over the First Source. Lane pilots call the northwestern horizon "the blank" and do not look at it long. The high **Eldorado pass** opens onto exactly this reach, which is why prospectors who descend from it so rarely file a second claim.

**The lesser touches:** **Draj's mud-shelf** (~100 ft down — shallow enough for wall-seeps to make mud; the ring's breadbasket and a standing moisture anomaly the wardens quietly watch) · the Guild's marked **crossing lanes** between the portages · and **Eldorado's rim pass**, where prospectors descend chasing rumors and mostly feed the horrors.

## DM systems summary

- **Travel:** Guild lane crossing = expensive and safe-ish; off-lane = shoal-reading (Survival vs. shifting routes) + horror ambushes + storm risk. The deeps: you cross them and remember nothing.
- **Encounter gradient:** arms and shallows = alive and hungry · the lanes = regulated and watched · open silt off-lane = empty and eerie · the northwestern Deeps = the Veil.
- **The clock:** every disturbance of the cycle — a big harvest, a rain-year, a warden shortage ([the Silence is pulling them south](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source.md)) — ratchets bloom pressure. The silt sea is the campaign's second infection clock, running quietly behind the Scarlands one.
