# Chapter 2a: The Old Woman

*She lives where the town stops and the pass has not quite started.*

The hut is stone and hide, above the last outfitter, where the street gives up being a street. The door faces the pass. Gray dust lies on the sill and she does not sweep it. A prospector's mule is tethered outside, and the prospector is nowhere.

She is old the way the desert people are old: not bent, just worn to the grain. Her eyes are blue all the way through. She does not wear a veil against the dust, and the dust does not seem to trouble her. She does not ask your name. She does not say whether her people cast her out or she walked down from the pass one day and stayed, and Eldorado has two stories and tells neither to strangers.

She looks at you for a while. Then she says:

> "You want to go up and ask. Everyone who comes to this door wants that. First you sit."

## The test

You sit a night outside the hut. No water. No fire. Below, the drink-houses of the long street go quiet one by one; above, the wall makes its sounds — the wind across the lip, the hiss of a silt-fall, things that are not wind.

In the morning she brings you a cup and does not give it to you yet.

> "Tell me what you heard."

There is no right answer. There is an honest one. If you make something up she will know, and she will hand you the cup anyway, and she will not take you.

*The DM rolls for the night. A hard night is not a failed night.*

## The price

She does not want coin. She wants a true thing about yourself that you have told no one. Not a secret that hurts someone else — a thing that is only yours. You say it to her, once, and she never repeats it, and from then on she knows it.

> "The desert will ask you for more than that. Think of this as practice."

## Choose

- **[Accept the price](3-The-Wall.md)** — say the true thing, and go up.
- **[Refuse and go back](1-The-Village.md)** — drink the cup, and walk down the long street.
