## scene: welcome [radio]

WANDERER: [warmly, a touch of wonder] Good white hours, friends, wherever the gray finds you — and tonight, for the first time in this show's life, it found me first. I am not at my radio. You're hearing me through the tower set at Midreach waystation, five days out on the Long Reach, with the lamps lit and the common room listening at the door. How did an unlicensed broadcaster get the use of a Guild tower? [beat] A courtesy was extended, friends. I didn't ask twice, and neither should you.

WANDERER: Tonight: the audit. Bellin and I settle a ledger four seasons old, on the air, with witnesses. Then a lesson I owe you, a letter I didn't expect, and at dawn — the part I've been promising. The blank. North past First Mark, and then west, where the charts go quiet. [beat] Pour the tea. Bellin's pouring mine, and writing it down.

## scene: silt-report [radio]

WANDERER: The silt report — and tonight, friends, I'm reading it straight off Midreach's own beacon log, which feels like borrowing a priest's notes. Winds west, always. One twenty-nine in the shade of this very tower. The lane south to Saltbone: clean, staked, and moving — the Cochlea took more freight this tenday than any since spring, and took it singing. The lane north to First Mark: clean. [pause] And past First Mark, where I'm headed, the log has one word, in a very old hand: "unstaked." [beat] That's the report. Stay between the markers, friends. I'll let you know what's past the last one.

## scene: the-audit [radio]

WANDERER: The audit! Friends, since episode one this man has claimed I owe for twenty cups of tea, and a certain Reckoner — on this very frequency — put the true figure at fourteen. Bellin: the floor is yours, and the floor is listening.

KEEPER: [easy swagger] And the floor keeps better books than you do. Twenty, Wanderer. Fourteen cups drunk on the porch, four carried up the tower stairs by my own hands, and two — [beat] — two poured for a man who sat right there and said "I shouldn't, I'm on the air tonight," and then drank them. Ol' Bellin counts what crosses the counter, not what gets confessed to.

WANDERER: [laughing] Friends, I have been out-audited at my own audit. Twenty it is, paid in full, in ceramic, on the counter — witnesses, note the sound.

SFX: a small handful of ceramic coins set down firmly on a wooden counter, clear distinct clatter, close-miked | 2

WANDERER: [beat] And now the other ledger, Bellin. The one the whole lane's been writing me about since spring. Ships putting in heavy and leaving light. Sums too small to see. You told me to come out and audit you myself. [quieter] I'm here.

KEEPER: [pause, the swagger set down] So you are. [beat] All right. You want to see the store-room?

WANDERER: You know I do.

KEEPER: Then come off the air first. Some doors don't open on frequency, Wanderer. Not even for you.

PAUSE: 1.5

WANDERER: [quiet, changed] ...Friends, we stopped the set for half an hour. I've seen the store-room. And here is every word I can give you, and I ask you to weigh how carefully I'm choosing them: the books balance. Nothing at Midreach is missing, and nothing at Midreach is stolen. What's here is *held* — and there is a difference, and by now you know the difference is the whole Code. [pause] It's not my door to open. But I'll tell you this much: it's the desert's. [beat] Bellin — the tea's paid. Keep the light on for me.

KEEPER: [warm, low] Always was, friend. Mind the markers going north. [pause] All of them.

## scene: the-lesson [radio]

WANDERER: Now the lesson, friends, because a listener wrote in asking the question I should have answered episodes ago. Quote: "Wanderer — what does 'answer the beacon' actually mean?" [beat] It means this. A beacon is a light at a fixed place — a marker, a tower, a registered flare with a name in the Guild's book. When one burns, every hull that sees it must turn and help, because out here the convoys are the rescue, and every crew knows the next burning light could be theirs. That's rule two, and it is the kindest law this continent has.

PAUSE: 0.9

WANDERER: And rule four is its shadow, and here is the whole of it, the way the old hands teach it: a beacon is light. It never speaks. [pause] If it speaks — if it calls your name, if it waves, if it hails you from open silt where nothing could be standing — it is not a beacon, and you do not stop. [beat] The crews at marker eighty-one knew that. Saltbone's lamps burning blue on a windless night — Vess knows it too, and said "it's nothing," and I've learned to hear what a keeper's "nothing" weighs. [quieter] I'm telling you this tonight, friends, because tomorrow I'll be past the last marker, and whatever shows me a light out there — I know the rule. Light, I answer. Voice... [pause] I keep my eyes on the road of my life, and I walk.

## scene: mail-run [radio]

PAUSE: 1.0

WANDERER: The mail, friends — one letter, and it was waiting for me here. At Midreach. Addressed in the hand you all know by now, in the excellent ink. [pause] I told no one I was coming here. [pause] Quote. "You'll want directions. There are none. You already know the rules of where you're going — you've said them every night for a season." [pause] "The fourth one is ours. We wrote it." [long pause] [very quiet] ...Friends, I have read that letter eleven times, and I will tell you the honest shape of what I feel, because this show has never lied to you: it is not fear. It's the feeling of a door you've knocked on your whole life, opening from the inside. [beat] The kettle is theirs now. I'm going to go say thank you for the rule.

## scene: outro [radio]

WANDERER: And that's the hour — and the season, friends, though the desert doesn't count in seasons and neither, anymore, do I. At dawn there's a skimmer waiting with a pilot who took the fare and asked no questions, which on this continent is the highest form of friendship money can buy. North to First Mark, where the light is kept and I intend to finally see by whom. Then west. [pause] Past the last marker, off the last chart, into the blank — where my correspondent is waiting, and where, if the letters are right, I've been before, for one hour, years ago, with the taste of blue on my tongue.

PAUSE: 1.0

WANDERER: [warmly] Keep listening to this frequency, friends. When I'm back, you'll hear it here first — everything, the whole account, no doors closed. And if the set stays quiet a while... [beat] well. You know the rules. Say them for me. [pause] Pay the rate. Answer the beacon. Drink your water in the shade. [very quiet] And stop for nothing that hails you from the gray. [pause] Thank you for a season of your evenings, friends. Hold the door open behind you. [warmly] Good night from channel forty-three.
