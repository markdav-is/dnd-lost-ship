# Eldorado — the Rim Town

Inland behind Tyr, at a high pass where the road climbs toward the cliff country. The last place to buy rope before the high country, and the first place to hear a lie about what's up there.

## At a glance

- **Size** A few hundred permanent residents; the transient population doubles it and turns over constantly.
- **Living** Two trades. **Outfitting** — rope, water-skins, dust-veils, climbing irons, mules, guides, all of it dear, because where else are you going to get it? And **trinkets**: the diggers work the old shafts above the pass and bring up whatever the deep gives them, and the town sells it by the tableful.
- **Reputation** Gold-fever with no gold. The name promises a city of gold; what Eldorado actually has is **hidden treasures** — the kind you find by turning things over on a stall. Prospectors have been going up from here for centuries; a few come back with salvage, most come back poorer, and some do not come back.

## What a visitor sees

One long street of outfitters and drink-houses, a chapel, a water-master's office, and a great deal of gear for sale second-hand — which is to say, sold by people who returned, and by the families of people who didn't. The wind off the heights carries fine gray dust that gets into food, eyes, and lungs; locals wear veils without thinking about it.

**And the tables.** Between the outfitters, and down every side alley, Eldorado is one long swap meet. Trestles and blankets heaped with small things: bent glass, buttons of a metal nobody can name, little engine-bones, charms, tokens, a lens, a key to no door — some of it ancient, dug out of the dark, and some of it knocked together last tenday by a local with clever hands and a straight face. Nobody labels which is which. Most of it is junk. People cross a continent for the rest.

## Traveler's notes

- Every guide in Eldorado claims to know the high country. Ask how many crossings they have made and who came back; the honest ones give small numbers.
- Sandwalker traders occasionally come down to barter. They are courteous, they do not drink, and they will not guide you up. Not for any price. Do not press it.
- Buy more water than you think you need. This is the standard advice and the graveyard is full of people who found it excessive.

- **Buying trinkets:** haggle, and buy what you like rather than what you're told is valuable. The sellers mostly don't know what they have, and the ones who say they do are the ones to watch.
- They say that if you know which table to stand at, and what to ask for, Eldorado sells stranger things than trinkets. Nobody who says this can ever tell you which table.
