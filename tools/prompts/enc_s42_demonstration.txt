The demonstration at S42 Medical Storage. Medium shot across a white lab bench. George Decay — tall dignified East Asian man in his late sixties, match the reference face, swept-back grey-black hair, cream ship jumpsuit with sleeves pushed up — wears a backpack rig: a squat pale-red cylinder repurposed from a ship's fire-suppression unit, harness straps made of cut and re-buckled grey gurney webbing, a rubber hose to a long brass-tipped wand. He is spraying a soft pale alkaline mist in a cone across a wire cage on the bench, his expression intent and quietly pleased, like a showman who has waited a month for an audience. A white sheet he has just pulled off lies crumpled beside the cage. Inside the cage: a rabbit-like creature from an alien garden — a large lop-eared rabbit with faintly mottled grey-green fur — pressed against the back wall of the cage, fur on end, mouth open mid-shriek, its eyes just clearing from a milky white film; a sad wilted lettuce leaf on the cage floor. A faint russet haze is dissolving out of the air around the cage where the mist hits it. Russet-gold fungal crust on George's left neck and jaw and left forearm, receding and healing. Background softly out of focus: medical storage racks, a synthesis rig of clear tubing, a boxy white worker robot on treads.
