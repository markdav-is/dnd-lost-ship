## scene: welcome [dry]

WANDERER: [warmly] Good white hours, friends, wherever the gray finds you — Wandering Aerun, channel forty-three, from the relay that shall remain nameless and the man who shall remain hydrated. Tonight, friends... [beat] I have been trying to arrange this program for three years, and I want you to appreciate what you're about to hear, because one of tonight's guests said yes immediately, and the other one said yes the way you agree to a tax.

WANDERER: The waystations! Three lights on three hundred and fifty miles of nothing — First Mark, Midreach, Saltbone. The only doors on the Long Reach, the only tea, the only beds, the only proof for five days at a stretch that anybody ever came this way before you and lived. You know Bellin of Midreach — everybody knows Bellin; he makes sure of it. And tonight, for the first time on any frequency: the keeper of Saltbone. [beat] Friends — these two run stations two and a half days apart on the same lane, and they have not spoken in twenty years. Nobody knows why. Tonight we find out, or we witness something. Either way: radio. Pour the tea.

## scene: silt-report [radio]

WANDERER: The silt report! [rustle of paper] Winds — west, friends, always west, and if you're new here, that joke gets funnier around your fortieth episode. Temperatures: one twenty-six on the Crest, one thirty-three at Midreach, sidings still declining, and honestly their silence is the most reliable measurement we have. Markers all standing, lane's clean — a good tenday on the Reach, and yes, I said it out loud, and no, I'm not superstitious, [dry] I'm just aware of the pattern. The Balic portage: arbitration continues. The word "continues" is doing a great deal of work in that sentence, friends. The porter gangs remain precisely, magnificently lawful — every basket weighed twice, every seal inspected like it owes them money — the freight remains stacked, and my advice remains the cheapest thing on this continent: pay. The porters. That's the report. Stay between the markers.

## scene: interview [radio]

WANDERER: All right. On the band from the middle light: Bellin, keeper of Midreach. And on the band — genuinely, friends, I checked the wire twice — from the southern light, twenty years of professional silence made flesh: Vess, keeper of Saltbone. Welcome, both.

KEEPER: [easy swagger] Evening, Wanderer. Evening, everyone listening. [beat] Almost everyone.

KEEPER2: [flat, gravelly] Wanderer. Get on with it.

WANDERER: [delighted, careful] So. Twenty years. Same lane, same Guild, same weather. Vess — I'll start with you, because Bellin starts with everything else. Saltbone. Paint us the picture.

KEEPER2: Saltbone is the last light before the Balic Saddle. When the wind runs, the silt-falls smoke over the rim and my lamps burn blue. I keep water, beds, and weather, same as any keeper. What I don't keep is patter. You want patter, stop two lights north — they pour it free up there, and it still costs too much.

KEEPER: [chuckles] She means me, friends. Twenty years, and I'm still the only thing she measures distance by.

KEEPER2: The Guild measures it. I just agree with the markers.

WANDERER: [gleeful] Friends, you're hearing history. Okay — I have to. You both know I have to. Twenty years, two stations, one lane. What happened?

KEEPER: [pause] ...You want to tell it, Vess?

KEEPER2: No.

KEEPER: [sighs] The hauler.

WANDERER: The — I'm sorry, the hauler?

KEEPER: The supply hulk, the year I took the station. Guild long hauler, ran the lane Raam-side down to Saltbone with everything Midreach ever ate, drank, or slept on. Ugliest hull on the gray and never once late. [beat] I named her Vess.

WANDERER: [long pause] [strangled] You named the boat—

KEEPER2: He named that barge after me, announced it at the Saltbone winter muster, in front of my crews, and then he stood there — [flat] — and he waited to be thanked.

KEEPER: [genuinely baffled, twenty years of it] It's an honor! Hulls are named for people you admire — that's tradition, that's the oldest tradition the lane has! She was magnificent! Never quit, never grounded, outlived three pilots—

KEEPER2: You told my crews she "rode low and smelled like the station."

KEEPER: [beat] ...The station smells fine.

WANDERER: [barely holding it together] Oh, friends. Twenty years. Two and a half days of gray between them, twenty years, and it's — [exhales] — it's a boat. It was always a boat. Vess — serious question, while I have you both, and then I'll let the lane have its silence back. The ships putting in light at Midreach. You're the next light south. What do you see?

KEEPER2: [long pause] I see what Bellin's not saying, which is a first, so enjoy it. Ships come past Saltbone riding higher than their manifests. That's his business and the Guild's. But the lane talks, Wanderer, and lately it talks in sums — small ones, steady ones, the kind somebody thinks are too small to see. [beat] Whatever you're holding up there, Bellin — hold it further from my lane.

KEEPER: [quiet, no swagger for once] ...Noted, Saltbone.

WANDERER: [softly] Friends, I want you to mark the time, because you heard it here: that was the first direct word between these two stations in twenty years. [beat] Bellin. Vess. The lane's lucky to have you both, and it'll never hear me say it again.

KEEPER: [swagger returning] Course it is. Mind the markers, Wanderer. And Vess — [pause] — the tea's still on at Midreach. Two and a half days. You know the lane.

KEEPER2: [long pause] [gravel, almost warm] ...Good night, Bellin.

## scene: mail-run [radio]

WANDERER: [quietly] Mail run, friends, and I'll be honest, anything after that is a footnote. [rustle of paper] First: several of you have asked — reasonably — why tonight was two keepers and not three. Where's First Mark? [beat] Friends, I asked. I ask every season. First Mark declined. First Mark always declines. Same hand, same ink, same four words: "The light is kept." [pause] [dry] I have interviewed a man about a mekillot grudge older than some of my listeners, but the northern light won't give me five minutes. File that wherever you file things.

WANDERER: Second — a drover writes: "Wanderer, my pay is reckoned by the week, but the Guild bills my board by the tenday. Am I being robbed?" [beat] [deadpan] Yes. Elegantly. The Reckoners standardized the tenday because the ledgers prefer round numbers, friend, and your foreman prefers the ledgers. Ten days' board, seven days' pay, and the difference is called overhead, which is a Reckoner word meaning "yours, formerly." Take it up at the muster. Take a friend.

## scene: notices [radio]

WANDERER: Notices! The Bitey report — Altaruk announces that Bitey the kank has been promoted. [beat] To mascot. Morale is up, the ceramic reserves are down, and the fort's quartermaster was quoted — actually quoted, friends — saying "we've stopped keeping the reward in coin." Wisdom. Next: Salt View confirms the position of "someone comfortable being eaten nightly" has been filled. [beat] Twice. No further questions were taken. And the Menders' clinic finishes its southern rotation at Saltbone this tenday — line before dawn, and with the silt-falls smoking, bring a cloth for your face. And look for the Hall's mark on the brow, so you never have to ask.

## scene: outro [radio]

WANDERER: That's the hour, friends, and what an hour. My thanks to Bellin — who has already written asking for a recording of the broadcast, presumably to frame the good parts — and to Vess of Saltbone, who I genuinely believe enjoyed herself, and who I will never, ever say that to. [warmly] Next time on Wandering Aerun: we go to the mountains. Salt View — the village that traded its chains for a stage, where the theater is the industry and the industry is thriving. I'll be talking to the director of the continent's most famous troupe about heroes, tyrants, and the noble art of being eaten nightly. [dry] For wages. It's a good one.

WANDERER: Until then, the old rules, all four: pay the rate. Answer the beacon. Drink your water in the shade. And stop for nothing that hails you from the gray. [beat, softer] Twenty years is a long silence, friends. Somebody out there owes somebody a first word. Go pay it. [warmly] Good night from channel forty-three.
