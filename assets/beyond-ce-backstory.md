# Beyond-Ce — backstory

*By Andy (natnerd15), as written on the D&D Beyond character sheet. Pasted in full 2026-09-22; the PDF export truncates it after "You have to know when to stop."*

Beyond-Ce has never been particularly interested in being a hero.

Heroes seek glory. They tell stories about themselves. They chase monsters because they want to be remembered for killing them.

Beyond-Ce has always been more practical.

If someone needs to get from one place to another, she can get them there. If the road is dangerous, she knows how to navigate it. If something comes looking for her passengers, she knows how to make it regret finding them.

And if someone is willing to pay enough, she'll take the job.

She's developed a reputation over the years as a reliable escort and guide. Merchants, travelers, adventurers, couriers, and occasionally people who would rather not explain why they need to leave town have all hired her. She doesn't ask many questions. She doesn't make promises she can't keep. Most importantly, she doesn't abandon her clients when things go wrong.

Her reputation can be summed up rather simply:

**If Beyond-Ce agrees to get you somewhere, you will probably get there.**

The important word is *probably*.

## The Road

Beyond-Ce learned early that traveling with other people requires more than knowing which direction to walk.

You have to know when to stop.

You have to know when to run.

You have to know which person in a group is going to panic first, which one will make a stupid decision, and which one will keep their head when everything goes wrong.

She became good at reading people.

She also learned that being the person responsible for everyone else means occasionally putting yourself between danger and the people who hired you.

She doesn't particularly enjoy fighting.

She simply accepts that sometimes it is part of the job.

Over time, she became known for being quiet and professional. She isn't unfriendly, but she doesn't waste words. She doesn't need to intimidate people because she rarely needs to raise her voice. There is simply something about the way she watches a situation that makes people think twice before causing trouble.

And despite her willingness to take dangerous work for money, she has one rule that she takes seriously:

**Once she accepts responsibility for someone, she sees the job through.**

That rule has gotten her into trouble more than once.

## The Night in the Woods

The job that changed her life began like any other.

A small group of travelers paid her well to guide them through a remote stretch of wilderness. They weren't particularly remarkable people, and Beyond-Ce didn't ask many questions.

The journey was supposed to take several days.

On the third night, they made camp.

The fire was burning normally.

The stars were visible.

The forest was quiet.

Then the fire went out.

Not slowly.

Not because of the wind.

One moment there was a fire, and the next there was nothing.

Beyond-Ce immediately knew something was wrong.

She ordered everyone to stay together.

Then the darkness moved.

She still isn't certain what she saw that night.

It looked like a creature, but calling it a creature doesn't feel quite right. It didn't move like something with bones or muscles. It seemed to slide between the trees, swallowing the light around it. The travelers panicked. One of them ran.

Beyond-Ce went after them.

Not because they were paying her.

Not anymore.

Because they were *hers*.

She found the traveler only moments later, frozen in terror.

The thing was behind them.

Beyond-Ce put herself between them.

She remembers drawing her weapon.

She remembers the darkness rushing toward her.

She remembers thinking, very calmly:

*This is going to hurt.*

Then everything disappeared.

There was no sound.

No light.

No ground beneath her feet.

For a brief moment, Beyond-Ce experienced something she still has difficulty describing. She was nowhere and everywhere at once. She could see the people she had been protecting, but they seemed impossibly far away. She could see the creature, but it seemed equally distant.

And then she saw something else.

Something watching.

She doesn't know what it was.

She only remembers the sensation of being noticed.

Then she was back.

She was standing several feet away from where she had been.

The creature was gone.

The traveler was alive.

And Beyond-Ce was lying on the ground with blood running from her nose and a darkness clinging to her hands.

She had survived something that should have killed her.

She never learned why.

## What Came Back With Her

After that night, things changed.

Beyond-Ce discovered that darkness no longer behaved normally around her.

She could see where others could not.

She could feel when something was hiding nearby.

Sometimes shadows seemed to move when there was nothing there to move them.

And occasionally, when she was frightened or angry, she could feel something cold and unfamiliar stirring inside her.

The first time she deliberately created darkness, she thought she was losing her mind.

The second time, she realized she could control it.

She eventually came to understand that whatever had happened that night had awakened something within her.

Shadow magic.

She doesn't know where it came from.

She doesn't know whether she was born with it.

She doesn't know whether the creature gave it to her.

And she doesn't know whether she brought something back from that place in the darkness.

She has deliberately avoided finding out.

For now, the magic is useful.

That's enough.

## The Hound

There is one part of her magic that bothers her more than the rest.

Sometimes, when she reaches deep into the darkness within herself, something answers.

A great black hound emerges from the shadows.

It obeys her.

It hunts the person she directs it toward.

It disappears when its work is finished.

Beyond-Ce calls it a tool.

She tells herself that is all it is.

But she remembers the creature from that night.

The shape is different.

The size is different.

The behavior is different.

And yet there is something about the hound's eyes that feels familiar.

Sometimes, when she summons it, the hound looks at her before turning toward its target.

Almost as if it recognizes her.

Almost as if it is waiting for something.

Beyond-Ce has never told anyone that.

## The Road Ahead

Years have passed since that night.

Beyond-Ce has continued working as a guide and escort.

Partly because it pays well.

Partly because she enjoys the freedom of being on the road.

And partly because she has discovered something she doesn't particularly like admitting:

She is more comfortable when she is responsible for someone else than when she is responsible only for herself.

When she's traveling with a client, there is always a destination.

There is always a job.

There is always something that needs to be done.

And perhaps most importantly, there is always someone she can protect.

She doesn't consider herself heroic.

She has escorted people she disliked.

She has transported people whose morals she questioned.

She has accepted coin from people she suspected were hiding something.

Money is money.

But there is a line she will not cross.

If she gives someone her word that she'll get them there, she will.

Even if it costs her.

Even if it means fighting something stronger than herself.

Even if it means putting her own life on the line.

Because that is the one thing Beyond-Ce knows about herself with absolute certainty:

**She may not be a hero.**

**But she does not leave people behind.**
