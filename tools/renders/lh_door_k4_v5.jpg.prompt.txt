A wide 16:9 painting in the same painted style as the reference images. We are looking through a rift torn in a tower: its glowing gold torn edges, wrapped in pale root-like mycelium cords, frame only the very left and right edges of the picture. Beyond, filling almost the whole frame, is a hostile desert, the Sea of Silt:

A vast, flat sea of fine pale-grey dust stretching to the horizon. On the left, tall ochre columnar cliffs with slow grey cascades of silt pouring over their crest. Standing out of the dust at many distances: giant black obsidian coral fungus, shaped like the black growth in the first reference image: squat, wide, rounded masses of many short, thick, upright branches packed tightly together and forking into clusters of stubby tips, like Ramaria coral mushrooms or staghorn coral, all faceted black volcanic glass bristling with jagged splintered obsidian shards and needle spikes, cracked with a dull gold glow, golden spores venting from the broken tips. No trees, no trunks, no single stems, no caps. The nearest are as big as houses, others shrink into the distance.

A harsh sandy wind: sheets of sand and grey silt streaming sideways across the plain, grit whipping off the coral, dust smoking off the dune crests. Far out in the middle distance, one long low curved ridge breaks the silt with a wake of disturbed dust, as if something enormous is moving underneath; nothing else of it is visible.

The sky is very bright and oppressive: a blinding, bleached, glaring white sky, a merciless sun-glare, heat shimmer, the air thick with hot pale dust. No clouds, no storm wall. Scorching, hostile, frightening, overwhelming. No people, no text.
