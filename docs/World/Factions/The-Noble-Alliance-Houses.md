# The Noble Alliance (Houses)

The seven great merchant houses of Aerun's city-states, coalescing in the Aura network's blind spots into something that is not yet — but wants to be — a Landsraad.

## Structure and Method

No formal charter; meetings happen only where the network cannot see (unimplanted couriers, dead zones, paper). The model is the ancient **Landsraad of legend** — a council of houses that collectively constrains a single ruler through unified economic and political pressure. They don't want Jak destroyed; they want to *negotiate* from strength.

## Assets

- Money, trade fleets, information brokers, safe houses across the city-states.
- **[Tomas Vel-Maret](/NPCs/Tomas-Vel-Maret)** — their agent in the Barrier Peaks, holding a copy of the Spásistren's sealed orders for the ship.
- What they lack: any counter to the Spásistren, and any presence inside the void. Hence their interest in the party.

## Honest Villainy

They are not liars — Tomas tells the party true things in service of a larger agenda. But "distributing the ship's technology" means distributing it **among the houses**. The Landsraad did not oppose the Emperor because they loved freedom; they wanted his power for themselves. Present them as reasonable, and let the players notice the shape of the deal.

## The Seven Houses

### House Wavir (Balic)

**Banner:** A silver jozhal on a blue field

House Wavir is one of the most powerful and respected merchant houses on the Ring Road. From their palatial headquarters in Balic, they dominate the shipment of grain, ceramics, and precious metals. Founded centuries ago by a former gladiator named Traxidos Wavir, the house has never forgotten its humble origins.

Wavir is unique among the great houses for its deep and abiding hatred of slavery. They rely entirely on paid labor, employing well-compensated mercenaries, psionicists, and agents. This ideological stance has earned them the loyalty of the common people but the suspicion of rival houses, who often accuse Wavir of secretly funding rebellions. Their massive wealth and heavily fortified outposts make them a dominant force in Ring Road politics, serving as a primary counterweight to more ruthless houses like Tsalaxa.

---

### House Tsalaxa (Draj)

**Banner:** A pair of glaring, bestial yellow eyes on a black field

Infamous throughout Aerun, House Tsalaxa rules the city-state of Draj through espionage, intimidation, and ruthless business practices. While they officially adhere to the Merchants' Code, Tsalaxa is widely known as a den of intriguers whose spies and assassins are second to none.

Tsalaxa specializes in securing valuable trading contracts through blackmail and sabotage. They maintain a vast network of underground tunnels in Draj and heavily fortified supply points along the Ring Road. *"Never cross the dragon or House Tsalaxa"* is a common proverb among Ring Road merchants. They are locked in a bitter, ongoing shadow war with House Wavir.

---

### House Inika (Gulg)

**Banner:** A plain gold circle on a black field

Operating out of the tranquil city of Gulg, House Inika is small by choice but disproportionately wealthy. They specialize in small, high-value cargoes such as kola nuts, exotic feathers, gemstones, and rare spices. Because their caravans are small, fast, and guarded by elite elven scouts, Inika can afford to maintain dozens of active trade routes simultaneously.

Inika's philosophy revolves around efficiency and non-confrontation. When challenged by a rival, Inika rarely meets force with force — instead withdrawing, changing tactics, and applying devastating economic pressure in unexpected areas. Led by the shrewd matriarch Andiama Inika, the house is famous for its fanatical internal loyalty.

---

### House Shom (Nibenay)

**Banner:** Three white dragonflies on a red-and-black diagonally divided field

House Shom is an ancient, immensely wealthy, but deeply corrupt house based in the decadent city of Nibenay. Centuries ago, Shom rose to power through brilliant leadership and unusual alliances with desert tribes and thri-kreen warriors. Today, however, the family has succumbed to sybaritic luxury.

The rulers of House Shom rarely leave their silken palaces, leaving actual management to their agents. This has led to rampant embezzlement and institutional rot. Shom caravans are massive, slow, and inefficient. Despite this decline, Shom's sheer volume of hoarded wealth and historical momentum keeps them seated at the table of the great houses. A quiet reform movement within the younger ranks of the family struggles to save the house before it collapses under its own weight.

---

### House M'ke (Raam)

**Banner:** A silver quill pen on a red field

House M'ke is a house of bizarre contradictions, ruling the increasingly unstable city-state of Raam. They are known for being exceptionally courteous and pleasant in person, yet utterly ruthless in business. They treat powerful rivals with intense respect while aggressively preying upon weaker houses.

Currently, M'ke is in a defensive posture. As the political situation in Raam deteriorates into near-anarchy, M'ke has withdrawn from many of its extended Ring Road routes to fortify its citadels. The house's greatest asset is its legendary hidden wealth — vast caches of coin and resources hoarded during prosperous years and hidden in secret vaults across Aerun. They are using this wealth to hire mercenary armies, attempting to wait out the chaos in Raam while remaining neutral in the growing rebellions.

---

### House Vordon (Tyr)

**Banner:** A black diamond on a red-brown field

House Vordon is a house of iron and conspiracy. Originally a dominant force that controlled the iron trade across the Ring Road, Vordon suffered a humiliating decline due to the erratic and destructive demands of Tyr's previous rulers. Bankrupted by forced contributions to massive public works, Vordon became a laughingstock among the other houses.

However, Vordon is pragmatic and ambitious. Under the leadership of Patriarch Thaxos, the house is secretly rebuilding its empire through dummy corporations and shadow companies, slowly amassing a secret army. Thaxos intends not only to restore Vordon's monopoly on the iron trade but to eventually seize political control of Tyr itself.

---

### House Stel (Urik)

**Banner:** A pair of crossed black scimitars on a white field

House Stel is the most aggressive and militaristic of the seven great houses. Founded by mercenaries and gladiators, Stel operates out of the warrior-culture city of Urik. Their business model is built around the spoils of war: weapons, slaves, stolen cargo, and mercenary contracts.

Stel's caravans are essentially moving fortresses. While they lack the subtle business acumen of Inika or the vast wealth of Wavir, they suffer fewer losses to raiders than any other house. Stel frequently hires out its elite guards and heavily armored wagons to other houses — including Tsalaxa, with whom they share a close, pragmatic alliance. The house is strictly organized along military lines, and its leaders view Ring Road commerce less as a market and more as a battlefield to be conquered.
