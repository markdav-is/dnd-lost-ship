# The Ring Road

The great highway that circles the whole continent, touching every city and most of the towns. Fourteen hundred miles of graded roadbed, wells, and waystops — the oldest continuously maintained thing on Aerun and the reason it is one place rather than seven.

## At a glance

- **Length** About 1,400 miles all the way round. A caravan making twenty to twenty-five miles a day circles the continent in **roughly two months**.
- **Distances between cities** Neighbors sit 100–150 miles apart, four to seven days of travel. Close enough to quarrel; far enough that marching an army is ruinous.
- **The traffic** Guild **mekillot argosies** — rolling fortress land-trains the size of villages — plus house caravans, pilgrims, couriers, herds, and everyone walking.
- **Who works an argosy** Three sorts, and everyone aboard is Guild: the **crew** who handle cargo and haul lines, the **drovers** at the helm and the brakes who drive the mekillot teams (a trade learned on the road), and the Academy's hooded **Navigators**, who ride openly, command the train, and keep the beasts calm. **The Guild crews free — there are no slaves aboard an argosy.** Slaves belong to the cities and work the ports, which is why cargo changes hands at the portage, and why the ports, not the road, are where freight goes slow.
- **Upkeep** Shared, grudgingly, by the families whose trade depends on it. Wells and waystops are maintained by whoever's territory they sit in, which means quality varies enormously between one stretch and the next.

## The circuit

Draj → Blackguard → Raam → Nibenay → Gulg → Amber Valley → Balic → Urik → Hollowstorm → Osgaker → Tyr → back to Draj. Eldorado sits on a short spur toward the high country; Gulg hangs off the road by a forest track, since the road does not enter the wood.

## Traveler's notes

- Walking alone is possible on the settled stretches and unwise everywhere else. Attach yourself to a caravan at any city gate or at Amber Valley, where hiring is done openly each dawn.
- The road is neutral ground by ancient custom. Feuds are not conducted on it. This holds well in the west, indifferently in the north, and not at all near the Mekillot Mountains.
- They say raiders who board a great argosy always find less aboard than the manifest promised, and that the crews leave a cup of water at the door of a sealed hold at dusk and it is gone by morning. *An argosy is never late* — people say it on the road the way they say things they have stopped questioning.
- Two shortcuts exist and both are silt crossings: [the Long Reach](The-Long-Reach.md) from Raam to Balic, and the shoal routes the desert tribes use and will not sell.
