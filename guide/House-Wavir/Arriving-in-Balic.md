# Arriving in Balic

*Read this before you reach the gate. The Council cannot help you once you have spoken at the table.*

## At the gate

At your **first gate on the continent**, and only the first, a clerk will ask you *"What's your business?"* The clerk means all of it. You will declare five things:

1. **Your business:** why you are on Aerun.
2. **Your goods:** what you carry to sell.
3. **Your metals:** every ounce, down to the buckles.
4. **Your material components:** everything a spell could be built from.
5. **Your exit city:** the port you will eventually leave Aerun by.

Every gate after this one only checks the registry. The registry is one registry, in every city, and it remembers.

### Declare the port you mean

The fifth answer is the one visitors get wrong and the one that matters most. **The bond follows the declaration, not the traveler.** Whatever the gate holds for you waits at the city you *named*, whether or not you ever arrive there. Change your plans halfway round the continent, and your sword and your spell pouch will sit in a Guild vault on the wrong coast, faithfully, forever.

Amendments are possible: a Guild factor, a fee, and a wait while the registry letters cross the continent. The road is long and the fee is not small. Know your route before you know your first gate.

### The scale: metal for water

All your metal (weapons, armor, buckles) is taken at the scale, logged, and carried under **Guild bond** to your exit city, where it is returned when you leave. In exchange, **the gate fills your water skins: water for metal, weight for weight**, at a rate posted daily beside the scale.

Northerners call this robbery until they have walked a tenday of the Ring Road. Out here, the metal was always a cook-pot and the water was always the treasure.

### The wax: your components

Material components are **confiscated, sealed in wax, and logged in triplicate**: one copy for the gate, one for the Guild, and one pressed into your hand. They travel under bond to your exit city and wait for you there. You cross Aerun without your magic and collect it on your way off the continent.

Do not call this theft in front of an Aerunite. Cargo bonded is returned. It is simply returned at the far end.

> *Nothing is taken. Everything is held. You leave the continent exactly as armed as you came to it.*

### The table: your coin

Gold need not travel to your exit city at all. The gate table will change it for local coin, at a rate a Reckoner set and a Reckoner audits. The rate does not move for you.

| You lay down | You take up |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 gold piece | 1 **ceramic** |
| 1 silver piece | 1 **bone** |
| 1 platinum piece | 1 **obsidian**, the black glass, the highest coin |

Lie at the table, and the registry remembers. Cast a spell openly inside a city with components you failed to declare, and no writ will save you.

## The heat, and the white hours

Aerun sits on the equator, and the sun stands straight overhead. Midday on the Ring Road passes **120 degrees in the shade**, and there is no shade.

The day has two halves: the working hours at dawn and dusk, and the **white hours** in between, when the cities close their shutters, the caravans stop, and anything still moving in the open is either dying or a lizard. Move at dawn, move at dusk, and in the white hours lie still.

The white hours are also when **contracts are read, revised, and signed**, because nobody can be traveling away from an agreement while the sun is up. If a factor wants your signature before noon, ask why.

Water is counted, not poured. You will be asked how much you carry before you are asked your name.

## Why nobody wears steel

Metal is **shunned, not forbidden**. A man in mail at 120 degrees is carrying his own grave, and a steel breastplate at noon is a cook-pot. Aerunites carry bone, chitin, horn, hide, and good obsidian: light, cool, and cheap enough to break. A traveler who arrives in steel is marked at once as rich, foreign, and about to learn something.

## Who runs Aerun

Three powers, and you need only remember three.

1. **The king in the south.** Jak, the Administrator, keeps a **Spáhus** in every city: the "seeing-house" of his Spásistren, part guard house and part embassy. Nothing is worshipped there. Most of the road calls it *Jak's watch-house*. They watch; they do not rule. (This guide reaches you over Jak's Aura network.)
2. **The seven families.** Merchant houses rule the seven cities outright: Wavir in Balic, Tsalaxa in Draj, Inika in Gulg, Shom in Nibenay, M'ke in Raam, Vordon in Tyr, Stel in Urik. There are no crowns on Aerun. A crown is an asset that cannot be sold.
3. **The Caravan Guild.** No house, no city, no master. The Guild's argosies haul everything on the Ring Road, and its skimmers are the only things that cross the silt. The Guild carries everyone or no one. See [The Caravan Guild](../The-Caravan-Guild.md).

## Things you will notice

- **Armies do not march.** When two houses have a dispute the ledgers cannot settle, they send **champions to the arena**, and the stakes go to the house still standing. Every city has an arena for this reason. The bouts empty the streets.
- **There are no gods.** *"Where other lands raise temples, Aerun raises schools."* The five **Great Schools** train the mind, and a School master is the most respected person in any city.
- **Magic is despised.** Ask any child what made the gray interior, and they will tell you: wizards did.
- **Everyone has a wild talent.** Nearly every soul born on Aerun carries some small trick of the mind. Do not gamble against an Aerunite until you have watched them play.
- **Blue eyes mean Spice.** Heavy use of the Spice, the desert's one true luxury, stains the eyes a deep, even blue. Blue eyes mean great wealth, a champion's provision, or the deep desert. A beggar with blue eyes is a story walking.
- **Balic pays wages; the other cities do not.** The rest of Aerun runs on slaves, and will explain the arithmetic to you without a flicker of shame. The Guild is the other exception: there are no slaves aboard an argosy.
