## scene: welcome [dry]

WANDERER: [warmly] Good white hours, friends, wherever the gray finds you — Wandering Aerun, channel forty-three, undisclosed, unlicensed, and after last episode, [chuckles] unexpectedly popular. The wire's been full all tenday. Yes, I heard it too. It was really them. And no, I don't know if she's made the trip for the tea — I know better than to ask.

WANDERER: Tonight we leave the lane, friends. Up into the Mekillot Mountains, to a village on the eastern face that I'd argue is the single most improbable place on this continent — and I say that owning a radio in a silt sea. Salt View! The village that traded its chains for a stage. Freed slaves, friends — folk who walked off the blocks and the fields with nothing, climbed into the rocks, and instead of turning bandit like every sensible desperate soul before them, they built... theaters. Plural. And it worked! The troupes of Salt View tour every city on the ring, they play the waystations, they've made two hardened silt crews cry on a porch at Midreach — episode one, look it up — and their spring circuit sells out in ceramic before the posters dry. Tonight: the woman who runs the most famous stage in the mountains. Pour the tea. Places, everyone.

## scene: interview [radio]

WANDERER: My guest tonight runs the principal stage of Salt View — thirty years with the troupe, director of the touring production of the Fall of Yaramuke, which is currently making grown drovers weep at a waystation near you. Madame Ossia. Madame — welcome to channel forty-three.

DIRECTOR: [grandly] Wanderer. I have notes.

WANDERER: [beat] ...On the show?

DIRECTOR: On the show. Your cold opens run long, your interval music is charming and under-rehearsed, and you sit on your best material — that business last episode with the two keepers? A lesser director would have milked that silence for three more minutes. [warmly] But the bones are good, Wanderer. Very good. You have a company here, whether you know it or not.

WANDERER: [delighted and slightly wounded] Friends, I've been reviewed. Madame — Salt View. For the listeners who've never made the climb: a village of freed slaves builds an industry out of theater, in the mountains, on a continent that measures everything in coin. Make it make sense.

DIRECTOR: [the grande dame settling in] It makes perfect sense, and I'll tell you why: we were the only ones who could afford it. Not in ceramic, Wanderer — in truth. Every city on this ring has stories it cannot say out loud. Draj cannot stage a play about tyrants; the Houses would count the seats. Balic can, barely, carefully, with the names changed twice. But Salt View? We have already been property. There is nothing left to threaten us with that we have not been. So we say the quiet things on a stage at altitude, and the cities pay handsomely to climb up and hear their own secrets performed by people they used to own. [beat] They call it entertainment. We call it the long invoice.

WANDERER: [quietly] ...The long invoice. Friends, that's the finest thing said on this frequency since we opened it. Madame — the Fall of Yaramuke. Your signature piece. A city erased a thousand years ago over quarry rights, and you've made it the most requested production on the ring. Why does that one land?

DIRECTOR: Because everyone in the audience knows a city could still burn, and no one in the audience is allowed to say so. We let them weep about the old fire so they needn't speak about the next one. That is what theater is for, Wanderer — grief, delivered under a name the gate will accept. [beat, then briskly] Also the second act has a collapsing tower and real smoke. People adore the smoke.

WANDERER: [laughs] There she is. All right — Madame, I cannot let you go without the question the entire mailbag demands. The casting notice. "Someone comfortable being eaten nightly." The position was filled — twice, your notice said. [carefully] Madame... what does the eating?

DIRECTOR: [fondly] His name is Boffo. He is a silt runner, hand-raised in the company since he was the size of a boot, and he is the finest scene partner I have directed in thirty years — hits his mark, never upstages, and takes direction better than any hero I've cast. The role is eaten nightly; the actor is merely gummed. Boffo has no teeth in front. He has, however, [beat] opinions about his billing, which is why the second hire. The first one is fine. The first one asked for a larger name on the poster than Boffo's. [flat] Boffo objected. We honor Boffo's objections.

WANDERER: [barely composed] The beast gets billing. Friends. The beast gets billing, and honestly? On this continent, that's just sound contract law. Madame Ossia — director, freedwoman, keeper of the long invoice — thank you for making the climb to the wire.

DIRECTOR: [grandly] Thank you for the platform, Wanderer. Do something about your cold opens. [beat] And come see the smoke.

## scene: mail-run [radio]

WANDERER: Mail run! [rustle of paper] First — and I've been instructed to read this exactly as written, and friends, I intend to honor that: [beat] "Wanderer. It's me. I'm comfortable. Boffo is a professional. Signed, eaten nightly." [pause] [warmly] Friend — that's the whole letter, that's the whole life, and I hope you get your name on the poster. Under Boffo's. Obviously.

WANDERER: Second: a great many of you have written about First Mark since last episode. Theories, friends. So many theories. The light is kept by a Navigator who lost the hood. The light is kept by nobody and the lamps trim themselves. The light is kept by the first keeper, still, and don't ask how long that's been. [beat] [dry] I am not going to speculate, because the last time I said I wasn't speculating, I was lying, and I've grown. What I'll say is this: I've asked First Mark for an interview every season for six years, and the same hand answers in the same ink every time. "The light is kept." [pause] Four words is an answer, friends. It's just not an answer to my question.
