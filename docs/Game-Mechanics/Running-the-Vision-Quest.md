# Running the Vision Quest

The DM's numbers for [The Vision Quest](../Aerun-Players-Guide/The-Vision-Quest.md), the player-facing branching story in which a visitor earns a wild talent from the desert.

The players' pages are written so that the prose says *the DM rolls* and never says what. Everything it does not say is here. Wild talents themselves are in the Dark Sun Player's Guide (3d6 table, p. 112) — see [Dark Sun 5e Rules](Dark-Sun-5e-Rules.md).

## Eligibility

- **PCs with no wild talent.** Anyone raised off Aerun. This is the intended case.
- **Natives** already rolled a talent at creation, and the desert gives once. A native who goes up anyway is asking for something other than a gift; allow a second quest only as a **story cost** (a name in a Lodge ledger, a Sandwalker debt, a price the guide sets that is not on this page). The second talent, if any, should cost the character something the first did not.
- Anyone who already has the **Wild Talent feat** (a School graduate) has been Tested and has a talent; the desert knows.

## The guides' tests

Failing a test costs **one level of exhaustion**, not the quest. The guide takes them anyway. A PC who refuses the test, or the price, goes back to Chapter 1.

| Guide | Test | Check | On a fail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The old woman (Eldorado) | A night without water; tell her what you heard | **Con save DC 12** | 1 exhaustion; she still asks the question, and the honest answer still counts |
| The hooded runaway (Salt View) | The salt flat at noon, in silence, behind him | **Wis (Survival) DC 13** | 1 exhaustion; **speaking** fails the test outright and he walks back alone |
| The kreen hunter (Bitter Well) | A day and a night at kreen pace | **Con save DC 13** | 1 exhaustion; the hunter waits, and says nothing about it |

The old woman's "what did you hear" is a roleplay beat, not a roll. If a player invents something, she knows, and she does not take them — they return to Chapter 1 with the cup and a closed door.

## The crossing (Chapter 3)

Fail = **1 level of exhaustion**. The PC reaches the top regardless.

| Way | Check |
| :--- | :--- |
| The night stair | **Wis (Survival) DC 13** |
| The day pass | **Con save DC 13** |
| The silt-edge | **Wis save DC 12** (reading the guide's reading) |

A party on one crossing rolls individually. Two levels of exhaustion before the Spice is a real state to arrive in, and the Spice save below does not care.

## The Spice (Chapter 4)

**Con save DC 12.**

- **Success** — the vision. Roll on the table.
- **Fail** — the vision, **+1 level of exhaustion**, and **the Tembo walks the edge of it.** Narrate the [tembo](../Bestiary/Aerun-Stat-Blocks.md#tembo) at the periphery of whatever the vision page describes: lion-sized, leathery, never closer, never gone. No mechanical effect beyond dread. It does not interact with the spirit beast. Use it once in the vision, once at the waking.
- **Natural 1** — the vision, **2 levels of exhaustion**, and **a craving**: the PC wants the Spice again. This is a DM hook, not a condition — play it as a thing the character notices in themselves at waystation markets and blue-eyed strangers. Let it go where it goes.

Then **roll 3d6 on the Chapter 4 table** — or let the table choose the talent, if that is the table's style. The PC turns to the vision page and reads it (or you read it to them).

### The gift

- The talent is the **wild talent** from the Player's Guide table, exactly as written there. The one-line summary on each vision page is a reminder, not the rule.
- The PC **chooses Con, Int, Wis, or Cha** as the talent's ability score, now, and it does not change (DC = 8 + proficiency + mod).
- **A faint blue ring in the iris.** Cosmetic. Aerun natives, Schools, Guild pilots, and Sandwalkers will notice. No one else will.
- **The spirit-beast flash** ("the first time each day you use the gift, you see it for an instant") is **flavor with no mechanics**. It is a thing the player describes; it is not a sense, a warning, or a familiar. If a player wants to make more of it, that is a story you are running, not a rule.
- The vision page for **Energy Beam** asks the player to choose a drake at the table; that choice is the beam's damage type and is permanent.

### Refusal (Chapter 5b)

A PC may refuse the gift after reading the vision. They wake without the talent and without the blue ring, with the full memory. They may return **not sooner than a year** later (in-fiction; at the table, when it makes sense), run the quest again with any guide, and **the beast and the talent are the same** — do not reroll. The desert made its offer once.

## Travel and supplies

- **5 to 7 days round trip** from the village: one to two days to the wall, a day on the wall, a night in the ring of stones, and back. From Eldorado the pass is closer; the wall is not kinder.
- Water per the Dark Sun rules (the Player's Guide ch. 5; the Campaign Guide's heat and thirst rules). The guide carries their own and will not share. Eldorado's standard advice applies everywhere under the wall: buy more than you think you need.
- The guides do not fight. If you want an encounter on the way, the [Bestiary](../Bestiary/Aerun-Beasts.md) has the Tablelands tables; keep it off the wall itself, and keep it out of the ring of stones.

## The prices, as hooks

The players' pages say *the guide's price comes due*. These are open threads — use them or let them lie.

- **The true thing** (old woman). She never repeats it. But she knows it, and the desert people talk to each other across distances that do not make sense. Some day, somewhere, a blue-eyed stranger may know it too.
- **The bone tube** (runaway). Sealed, light, addressed to "any waystation" on the Long Reach. The PC swore not to open it or ask. What is in it, who the water-master sends it on to, and whether the Guild learns who carried it are yours to decide. The runaway will not be in Salt View when they return.
- **The first use** (kreen hunter). The hunter said *I will know*. Decide whether that is true. If the PC's first use of the gift is for themselves, nothing happens — at first.
