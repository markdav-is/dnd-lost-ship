Eye-level view from the lake shore at the west side of the inner garden, looking east along a 10-foot-wide straight metal footbridge with low rails that runs out across still black water to a small island about 150 feet away. The island is a mound of lush foliage and an octagonal low wall, with a slender pale lighthouse tower rising from the centre, its lower half wrapped in russet fungal growth like bark, its lamp turning, a pale beam sweeping slowly through heavy orange spore haze that hangs over the whole lake and glows where the sunlight hits it. On the muddy bank in the foreground, battered and crushed old robots — pale 1970s-style boxy machines, dented, half-sunk in reeds and weed. Small luminous fish glint under the surface near the bank; a long ripple crosses the water near the bridge as if something very large has just moved below. Bright warm overhead light from the sun-lamp far above, the rim of jungle on the far shores, the ribbed ceiling high over everything. Quiet, beautiful, and wrong.
