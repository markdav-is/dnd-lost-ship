# The Invisible Infection

Copper's theory: the mold's visible spores are only the part that can be seen, cut out, and burned. Something else is changing how people *think* — and it may be able to ride the Aura network itself.

## What the players have heard

- [Copper](../../NPCs/Copper-the-Surgeon.md), at Frostwatch: locals who were never physically infected are "different" — making decisions they wouldn't have made, protecting the infection without knowing it. *"What if it can ride a signal? What if it's already inside the network and nobody knows it yet?"*
- [Valerius](../../NPCs/High-Sister-Valerius.md), closing the council: **three separate trusted sources** have independently arrived at the same theory — which is either evidence it's true, or evidence the infection is already doing exactly what they fear.
- The Aura network has registered **anomalies** in affected regions — signals matching no known pattern, as if something is trying to communicate through the infrastructure.
- The infected deer herd on the descent acted as *scouts*; George: "The mold doesn't just infect — it networks."

## DM guidance

**Leave this thread unresolved.** The DM decides — possibly never on-screen — whether the invisible infection is real or the paranoid projection of a man who has seen too much. Either way it must feel credible. It is the most cosmically horrifying possibility in the campaign and should haunt every subsequent session:

- The Latent infection stage already causes victims to feel *unusually calm* ([infection rules](../../Game-Mechanics/Russet-Mold-and-Infection.md)).
- Symptomatic victims develop a *compulsive urge to protect infected creatures* — behavioral change **before** visible growth. Copper's observations have a mechanical basis.
- If real, the Spásistren — thirty minds hard-wired into one network — are the perfect vector, and Jak himself is the final host. Valerius severing her implant may retroactively become the most important act in the campaign.
