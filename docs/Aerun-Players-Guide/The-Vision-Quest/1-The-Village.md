# Chapter 1: The Village

*You are in a town under the wall, and you are asking the wrong people.*

The Rim Wall is the horizon. It has been the horizon for days. Ochre, columned, two thousand feet of it, and above the lip a gray line that does not move.

You have come to one of the places where the road ends and the climb begins. You have told someone what you want. They have laughed, or gone quiet, or named a price. Then every one of them has said the same thing, in the same flat voice, as if it were a toll:

> *"The desert does not give to those who were sent. It gives to those who went."*

They mean: nobody hires this out. Nobody can go for you. You walk up yourself, or you do not.

## The asking

Whoever you ask, the shape is the same. You pay to be pointed. The pointing is not the guide; the guide costs more, and the guide's price is never coin.

- **In [Eldorado](../Gazetteer/Eldorado.md)** you ask the pass-keeper — the water-master's man at the top of the long street, who logs every prospector going up and strikes a line through the ones who do not come down. The price of a pointing is the truth about how much water you are carrying, and buying what he says you are short. He does not sell guides. He nods up the slope, past the last outfitter, to a hut that faces the pass.
- **In [Salt View](../Gazetteer/Salt-View.md)** you ask the pit-boss — an ex-gladiator who runs the troupes' sparring yard and bets on it. The price is a bout. You need not win. You need to last long enough to be worth the time. Afterward he tells you where the hooded man sleeps, and that you did not hear it from him.
- **In [Bitter Well](../Gazetteer/Bitter-Well.md)** you ask the well-keeper — one of the dwarven families, civil and tired, who has heard every joke about the name. The price of a pointing is a day's work on the windlass while the shaft refills. The keeper nods toward the scrub, where something tall stands very still.
