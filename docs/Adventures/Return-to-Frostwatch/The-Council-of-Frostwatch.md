# The Council of Frostwatch

*Return to Frostwatch, Act III — the decision scene, in play order. Read top to bottom at the table; lore and motivations are at the bottom.*

The Great Hall, second bell: feasting tables replaced by a single circular table of dark wood, chairs at precise intervals. A fire burns. The room still feels cold.

## 1. McReady in the corridor

> *McReady straightens when he sees you. He is wearing clean clothes, which somehow makes him look more dangerous than when he was covered in frost and blood. He gives you the same rare, genuine smile you have seen before — the one that means he is about to ask you to do something difficult.*
>
> *"Good. You're all here." He falls into step beside you without waiting to be invited. "Before we go in, there are people at that table you haven't met. I'm going to introduce you. Let me do the talking for the first minute. After that, say whatever you need to say."*
>
> *He pauses at the door. "One more thing. The High Sister is Jak's voice in this room. But she's also the smartest person in it. Don't confuse those two things."*
>
> *He pushes the door open.*

## 2. The council begins

> *The Great Hall is quieter than you have ever heard it. The fire crackles. The wind presses against the shuttered windows. At the circular table sit the people who will decide the fate of the Barrier Peaks.*
>
> *At the head — or perhaps there is no head at a round table, and that is the point — sits High Sister Valerius. She is older than you expected, her hair silver-white, her face deeply lined. But her eyes are extraordinary: sharp, patient, and utterly without warmth. Behind her left ear, the blue light of her Aura implant pulses with slow, steady rhythm. She is connected. She is being watched.*
>
> *Copper sits across from her, his hands wrapped around a cup of something hot. He does not look at the High Sister. He looks at the table.*
>
> *And then there is the stranger. Kaelen al-Hajra sits in the chair furthest from the fire, her bone-handled knife placed handle-forward on the table before her. She is watching the doors. She does not look at you when you enter. She already knows you are there.*
>
> *McReady takes the chair nearest the door — the one with the clearest view of everyone at the table. He does not sit so much as settle, the way a man does when he has been in enough rooms like this to know exactly what they cost.*
>
> *Two Spásistren guards stand at the doors. The blue lights behind their ears pulse in perfect synchrony with Valerius's.*
>
> *Valerius says nothing. She waits.*
>
> *McReady speaks first. "High Sister." He nods to Valerius. "These are the people who went into the ship. They came back." He lets that sit for a moment. "Copper you know. The woman at the far end is Kaelen al-Hajra. She came from Aerun. She has something to say that you need to hear." He looks at Valerius. "I'll vouch for all of them."*
>
> *Something passes between McReady and Valerius that is not quite agreement and not quite challenge. Then she looks at the party.*
>
> *"Tell me what you found," she says. "Everything."*

## 3. The report

The party recounts the ship. Valerius listens without interruption, then asks exactly three questions:

1. *"Is there a central intelligence directing the infection, or is it autonomous?"*
2. *"Did you find any evidence of a cure or a kill-switch within the ship's systems?"*
3. *"Is the ship intact enough to be entered again?"*

## 4. The Severing

> *"We are alone," she says.*
>
> *The guards turn without a word and leave the hall. The heavy doors close. The bar drops.*
>
> *Valerius reaches up and presses her fingers against the skin behind her left ear. Her touch is practiced, precise — a sequence of points, like a combination. The blue light flickers. It dims. It goes dark.*
>
> *For a moment, she simply sits with her hand at her neck, her eyes closed. When she opens them, something has changed. The metallic quality is gone from her voice. She sounds, for the first time, like a person rather than a function.*
>
> *"Jak cannot hear us now," she says. Her voice is quiet, almost reverent in its heresy. "I have severed the connection. What we discuss in this room will not become part of the network's memory." She pauses. "I do this because I believe the Administrator's judgment may be compromised by the scale of what you have described. To the sisters, he is infallible. But you and I know he is a man. And he sees the infection as a political problem. I believe it may be something older than politics."*
>
> *She looks at each of you in turn. "I am going to tell you what I know. Then you are going to help me decide what to do about it."*

## 5. Valerius's revelation

Say in your own words: the infection spreads faster than public reports — **three villages quarantined, two silent, scouts unreturned**. The network registers **anomalies** in affected regions, as if something is attempting to communicate through the infrastructure. She doesn't know if the infection is intelligent; she suspects worse — *purposeful without being intelligent, the way a river is purposeful without being aware.*

## 6. Kaelen names it

> *She does not stand. She does not raise her voice. She simply places one hand flat on the table and says:*
>
> *"We call it Rajaat."*
>
> *The room is quiet. She continues.*
>
> *"In the deep desert of Aerun, there is a place that has no name on any map made by the coastal cities. The tribes who live there — my people — call it the First Source. The place where it began. The place where it has always been."*
>
> *"Aerun was not always a desert. There were forests. Rivers. Cities that are now buried under a hundred feet of sand. The infection came first. It spread the way yours is spreading now — slowly, then all at once. The animals changed. The trees changed. The people changed. And then the people who understood what was happening made a decision."*
>
> *She looks at each face at the table.*
>
> *"They made a desert. On purpose. They killed the land to save the world. No water, no organic matter for the spores to attach to, no host for the bloom. The desert is not a natural feature of Aerun. It is a quarantine. It is the only containment we have ever found that works. And it has held for a thousand years."*
>
> *She pauses. "What you have in your mountains is a second Rajaat. A second source. And unlike ours, it is sitting in a valley full of trees, animals, rivers, and people. Everything the spores need to bloom without limit."*
>
> *"Your desert does not exist yet. And creating one large enough to contain what is in that ship would take generations and kill everything between here and the sea."*

**Beat:** Valerius goes still. Copper stares at his cup. McReady looks at the ceiling. What follows is no longer politics — it is planetary triage.

## 7. Path One — Containment: Seal the Ship

![High Sister Valerius addresses the council](../../.attachments/council_valerius.jpg)

> *"We seal the mountain passes. The alchemical charges in the wagons are sufficient to trigger controlled avalanches at seven key points. We establish a permanent quarantine perimeter. We post Spásistren at every viable approach. Nothing leaves. Nothing enters. And we send a team back into the ship — not to fight, but to seal it. Close the hull breaches. Lock the internal bulkheads. Contain the source inside the vessel itself. The ship has been sealed before. It can be sealed again."*

**Valerius:** "Sealing the ship buys time. The hull is alien alloy — if we can close the breaches, the infection loses its vector to the outside world. The mountain quarantine holds the perimeter. We are not solving the problem. We are buying ourselves the time to find a real solution. Weeks, perhaps months. Possibly longer if the ship's own systems can be engaged to assist containment."

**Copper:** *"Time for what? The moment one spore finds a crack in that hull, we're back where we started. And every day we wait, it spreads further into the groundwater, the soil, the animals. Sealing the ship is a door. Doors can be opened. Doors can rot. What happens when the seal fails and there's no one left who knows how to go back in?"*

**Kaelen:** *(quiet a moment)* *"My ancestors tried containment first. They built walls. They burned the borders. They posted guards."* *(looks at the table)* *"The walls held for eleven years. Then one spring the bloom came from beneath the walls, not through them. The spores had been in the groundwater the whole time."* *(pause)* *"Sealing the ship is not nothing. It is better than nothing. But it is not a solution. It is a delay. And delays end."*

**McReady:** *(sets down his cup)* *"There are still people in those mountains. Hunters. Hermits. Travelers who don't know the passes are closing."* *(to Valerius)* *"You'd be sealing them in with it. I want that said out loud before we vote."* *(pause)* *"That said — if sealing the ship gives us time to find a cure, I'll carry the charges in myself."*

## 8. Path Two — The Purge

![McReady speaks to the council](../../.attachments/council_mcready.jpg)

> *"We have the means to call a strike from the orbital platforms. A localized incendiary deployment sufficient to sterilize the valley. Everything burns. The ship, the infection, the biomass — all of it. We end it today."*

**Valerius:** "This is the most certain short-term solution. It is also irreversible. The ship's technology would be lost permanently. The valley would be uninhabitable for a generation. And we would need to be absolutely certain that the infection has not already spread beyond the blast radius — because if it has, we burn the mountains for nothing and the problem continues elsewhere. There is also a second consideration." *(glances at Kaelen)* "If this infection has a First Source in Aerun that has been contained for a thousand years, destroying this second source does not address the original. It only removes the immediate threat."

**Copper:** *(a long silence, then quietly)* *"Do it. Do it now, before we talk ourselves out of it. Every hour we debate is another hour it spreads."*

**Kaelen:** *"If the fire is hot enough and the timing is right, it works for this source."* *(pause)* *"We did not have orbital platforms. We did not have the option. If you have it, do not dismiss it because it is ugly. Ugly solutions are still solutions."* *(to McReady)* *"I am sorry about your mountains. But Aerun had mountains too."* *(a beat)* *"But understand what you are choosing. You are choosing to live with Rajaat still in the deep desert, still contained only by sand and worms and the prayers of people the coastal cities have never met. You are not curing anything. You are only removing the second outbreak."*

**McReady:** *(long quiet; voice flat)* *"You would burn the mountains. Everything our people built up here — three hundred years of it."* *(to Valerius)* *"Jak would want the ship intact if possible. That's not an order. It's context. But I want it on record that I said what burning this place means."* *(to Kaelen)* *"And I heard what you said about the first source. So even if we burn this valley clean, the problem isn't over. It's just quieter."*

## 9. Path Three — The Cure

![Kaelen al-Hajra addresses the council](../../.attachments/council_kaelen.jpg)

> *"You return to the ship. You go deeper than you have gone. The ship's medical and research levels — wherever they are — may contain the means to synthesize a cure, or to trigger a system-wide decontamination. You find it. You use it here. And if the cure works here, it may work on Rajaat itself — the First Source in Aerun. Not just a delay. Not just a quarantine. An end."*

**Valerius:** "This is the most dangerous option. It gives the infection more time to spread while the team is inside. It risks the lives of the only people who have successfully navigated the ship. But if it works, it solves the problem completely — not just the second source in these mountains, but potentially the First Source in Aerun that has been contained for a thousand years by nothing more than sand and faith. The ship's technology may be the only thing in the known world capable of producing a true cure. And the Administrator's long-term plans for that technology make this option strategically irreplaceable."

**Copper:** *"You're asking them to go back into the void. You're asking them to trust that there's a cure in there. There might not be. There might be nothing in there but more things that want to eat them. And while they're in there looking, the infection keeps spreading."* *(to the party)* *"I'm not saying don't go. I'm saying know what you're walking into."*

**Kaelen:** *(a long look at the party)* *"This is the path Aerun never had. We did not know what was inside the ruins until it was too late to go in and come back out. By the time we understood what Rajaat was, the desert was already the only answer left."* *(pause)* *"You have already been inside. You already came back. That is not nothing. That is the only advantage Aerun never had."* *(leans forward)* *"And if you find a cure — a real cure, not a seal, not a fire — then what has been locked in the deep desert for a thousand years does not have to stay locked forever. My people have been the wardens of Rajaat for generations. A cure would free them too."*

**McReady:** *(directly to the party)* *"I wouldn't ask you to go back in. But I'm not going to tell you not to, either."* *(pause)* *"You know what's in there better than anyone in this room. Including me."* *(glances at Kaelen)* *"And if she's right — if a cure fixes both sources — then this isn't just about the Scarlands anymore. This is about Aerun. This is about the whole world."*

## 10. The debate & the question

![Copper the Surgeon makes his case](../../.attachments/council_copper.jpg)

The council erupts. Copper and McReady argue. Valerius watches, and does not intervene until the argument runs its course.

> *Valerius waits until the room is quiet again. Then she folds her hands on the table and looks at you with those extraordinary, patient eyes.*
>
> *"You have walked those halls. You have seen the void. You have fought what lives in it and survived." She pauses. "I have read reports and received signals and interviewed survivors. You have been there."*
>
> *"What is your counsel?"*

**If asked, Kaelen's vote:** *"The cure. Because it is the only path that ends it rather than delays it or destroys everything around it. And because a cure does not just fix what is in your mountains. It fixes what is in mine. My people have been the wardens of Rajaat for a thousand years. We did not choose that. A cure would give us a choice for the first time."*

## 11. Valerius's final word

> *As the council rises, Valerius remains seated. She is looking at the fire.*
>
> *"One more thing," she says, without turning. "Copper's theory — about the invisible infection, the one that rides the mind rather than the flesh." She pauses. "I have heard similar theories from three other sources in the past month. All of them people I trust. All of them people who have been close to the affected zones."*
>
> *She finally looks up. "I do not know if the infection can ride the network. I do not know if it can alter thought without leaving physical traces. But I know this: the fact that three separate, intelligent people have arrived at the same conclusion independently is either evidence that the theory is correct, or evidence that the infection is already doing exactly what they fear."*
>
> *She stands. "Sleep well."*

## Deeper knowledge (DM background — not for reading aloud)

- **Triage, not a vote.** The paths are not mutually exclusive: seal now → pursue the cure; attempt the cure → fall back to the purge. The council asks for a *direction*. Whatever the party chooses, Valerius accepts it, reconnects, and reports to Jak in terms that protect the party's agency — never mentioning the severing. Full decision reference: [The Lighthouse Dilemma](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Lighthouse-Dilemma.md).
- **[Valerius](../../NPCs/High-Sister-Valerius.md):** the severing is heresy, conviction, and a *test* — she watches how the party behaves unobserved. She does not know (or does she?) how neatly "seal the ship" matches Jak's sealed orders.
- **[McReady](../../NPCs/McReady.md):** party's sponsor, the mountain folk's advocate, and Jak's quiet reporter — the only one advocating nothing. Both his signals are true; neither is the whole truth. Confronted about the dis-trans bat: *"Jak needed to know you arrived safely. That's all it was."*
- **[Kaelen](../../NPCs/Kaelen-al-Hajra.md):** her rings of disclosure — the Rajaat story (told here) · the defiler method and the [Order of the Sere](../../World/Factions/The-Order-of-the-Sere.md) (only if pressed/private) · the worms (half-named in her purge response) · the Spice (**never**). Pressed past her limit: *"It is managed."* See [The First Source](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-First-Source.md).
- **[Copper](../../NPCs/Copper-the-Surgeon.md):** his invisible-infection theory is the campaign's cosmic-horror thread — [leave it unresolved](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Invisible-Infection.md).
- **The unspoken paths:** Jak's sealed orders ([Tomas Vel-Maret](../../NPCs/Tomas-Vel-Maret.md)) and the biological-containment long shot only Kaelen could propose — both in [The Lighthouse Dilemma](../../Campaign/Plot-Threads/The-Lighthouse-Dilemma.md) DM secrets.
